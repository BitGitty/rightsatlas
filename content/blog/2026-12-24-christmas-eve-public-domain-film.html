It's Christmas Eve, the night this story is set on, so here is a film to match: is The Night Before Christmas (1905) public domain? Yes, and so is the poem behind it. This is one of the cleanest public-domain Christmas pieces we have checked. Watch the audio track, though, before you post it anywhere.

Why the film print is free

The Night Before Christmas (1905). Edwin S. Porter directed this short for the Edison Manufacturing Company, released December 16, 1905. Works published in the US in 1930 or earlier are now public domain by term, and the Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain's Public Domain Day 2026 page confirms the rolling cutoff has already passed 1930 this year, so a 1905 release is well past its term, with no renewal search even needed. See the Duke CSPD Public Domain Day 2026 page.

The other layers

  • Story: public domain. The film follows Clement Clarke Moore's poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas," first published anonymously in 1823, long past any US copyright term, so there is no separate rights holder standing behind the words on screen.
  • Score: partly protected. The film was silent on release. The Public Domain Review's online copy carries a later soundtrack built mostly from old cylinder recordings, and the same site also offers a silent version. Any added soundtrack on a modern copy is a separate, later work, so pick the silent copy if you plan to add your own music. See The Public Domain Review's page for this film.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have no evidence on file for this layer.
  • Restorations: not verified. We have not checked whether any modern release of this short adds new titles, tinting, or other protected material.

A little history

According to Wikipedia, this is the first film adaptation of Moore's poem. Porter's staging follows it scene by scene: Santa readies his workshop, a city household hangs stockings, the sleigh makes its journey, and Santa comes down the chimney to leave presents, with Harry Eytinge playing Santa alongside live reindeer.
