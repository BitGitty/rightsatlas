Is the 1922 Robin Hood public domain? Yes. Douglas Fairbanks's swashbuckler is free to use in the US, and so is the legend it is built on. This is not the 1938 Errol Flynn film of almost the same name; that one is a separate, still-copyrighted Warner Bros. picture.

Why the film print is free

Robin Hood was published in the US in 1922. Every US work from 1930 or earlier is public domain by age under the pre-1978 copyright scheme, with no renewal check needed, so the print is verified public domain (Cornell Copyright Information Center). That bright line is the simplest rule on this site: anything published in the US in 1930 or earlier is free today, full stop.

The other layers

  • Story: verified public domain. Robin Hood is medieval English folklore, documented in ballads by the 15th century. No author owns it; it has been free for centuries.
  • Score: partially protected. The 1922 print carries no recorded music of its own. Commercial home-video releases, including Kino Lorber's, bundle a newly composed score and sound recording that are separately copyrighted.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A faithful restoration of public-domain images adds no new copyright by itself. Kino Lorber's new score and any added material on its releases are protected on their own, and ripping a copy-protected disc raises a separate DMCA concern.
  • Trademark: not verified. "Robin Hood" the character is not protectable on its own, and we found no active franchise trademark in this research pass, but we did not run a full USPTO search. Commercial franchise plans should run their own check before relying on that.

A little history

Fairbanks produced and starred in the film, directed by Allan Dwan and distributed through United Artists. It premiered at Grauman's Egyptian Theatre on 18 October 1922, the first film ever to have a Hollywood premiere. Built on a budget of roughly $930,000, it went on to earn $2.5 million at the box office (Wikipedia).
