Is The Golem (1920) public domain in the US? Yes. The German Expressionist horror classic's maximum US term ran out before copyright restoration could ever reach it, so it came out free by ordinary term expiry.

Is The Golem free to use?

The Golem was published in Germany in October 1920. Germany is a Berne Convention country, and the film was still protected there as of 1996 — co-director Carl Boese's life-plus-70 German term runs to 2028. That might suggest the URAA (17 U.S.C. § 104A, effective 1 January 1996) restored its US copyright. It didn't: URAA restoration only revives whatever US term a foreign work would otherwise still have had on 1 January 1996, and works published before 1923 maxed out at a flat 75-year US term. For The Golem, 1920 plus 75 is 1995 — the term had already run out on 31 December 1995, one day before URAA took effect. The film was never restored; it has been public domain in the US since 1 January 1996 by ordinary term expiry, the same pre-1923 rule Duke's Center for the Study of the Public Domain distinguishes from the longer term that applies only to 1923-and-later works.

What about the music in The Golem?

The story is free too. The screenplay by Paul Wegener and Henrik Galeen was first published with the 1920 film, so its US term also expired in 1995. The source novel, Gustav Meyrink's Der Golem (1915), is listed by Project Gutenberg as public domain in the US, and the Golem legend itself is ancient Jewish folklore with no copyright at all.

The score is the layer to watch. The Golem was shown silent, with live accompaniment, and no synchronized recording was ever made for it. Every commercial release bundles a newly commissioned score instead: the 2018 Friedrich-Wilhelm-Murnau-Stiftung restoration, on Kino Lorber (US) and Eureka Video (UK) Blu-ray, pairs the film with four separately owned scores, by Stephen Horne, Admir Shkurtaj, Lukasz "Wudec" Poleszak and Cordula Heth. None of those are free. A faithful restoration like that one adds no new copyright to the images themselves, but ripping a copy-protected disc can still run into the DMCA's anti-circumvention rule. Trademark status was left undetermined; no search for live marks has been done.

What's the story behind The Golem?

Directed by Paul Wegener and Carl Boese, the film stars Wegener as a towering clay figure summoned by a rabbi to defend Prague's Jewish ghetto. Hans Poelzig's cave-like sets and the shadow-heavy cinematography of Karl Freund and Guido Seeber gave it a visual style that James Whale's Frankenstein would echo a decade later.
