Is "When Irish Eyes Are Smiling" public domain? "When Irish Eyes Are Smiling" has been a Saint Patrick's Day staple for over a century. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1912, so it became free on 1 January 1988 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

The evidence

Wikipedia says the song, with music by Ernest Ball and words by Chauncey Olcott and George Graff Jr., was first published in 1912. IMSLP's catalog lists the 1912 M. Witmark & Sons first edition, agreeing with that date. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1912, that is 1988.

Recordings are a different right

A free song is not a free recording. Each recording of "When Irish Eyes Are Smiling," old or new, carries its own separate copyright on its own clock. If you need the song for a video or a new release, perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

It is remembered today as a Saint Patrick's Day standard (source).
