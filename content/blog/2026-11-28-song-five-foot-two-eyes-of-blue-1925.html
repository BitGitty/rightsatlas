Is "Five Foot Two, Eyes of Blue" public domain? "Has anybody seen my girl?" This jaunty question is the real title of a 1925 novelty song most people just call "Five Foot Two, Eyes of Blue." Here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was copyrighted in 1925, so it became free on 1 January 2021 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

The evidence

The music is by Ray Henderson, with lyrics by Sam M. Lewis and Joe Young. Wikipedia states the song "was copyrighted on October 19, 1925." The songwriting-credits site musicanet.org agrees, dating both the music and the lyrics to 1925. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1925 that is 2021.

Recordings are a different right

The song's own copyright has run out, but any particular recording of it is a separate copyright with its own clock. A 1920s dance-band disc and a brand-new cover both carry their own protection, even though the song itself does not. If you need the tune for a video, the safest path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

The song gave its name to the 1952 Universal-International film Has Anybody Seen My Gal?, starring Rock Hudson and Piper Laurie, which also features the tune (source).
