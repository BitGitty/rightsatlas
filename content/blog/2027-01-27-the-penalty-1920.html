Is The Penalty public domain? Yes. Lon Chaney's 1920 crime melodrama is free in the US, and so is the novel it was based on.

Why the film print is free

The film was published in 1920. As Duke's Center for the Study of the Public Domain explains, no published work entered the US public domain in 2016 because the longer term that applies to later works only reaches works from 1923 onward — 1923 works were first to enter, on 1 January 2019. A 1920 work predates that range entirely: it instead carried a flat maximum US copyright term of 75 years and was already in the public domain by 1 January 1996, so the film print is public domain in the US.

The other layers

The story is free too. The film is based on Gouverneur Morris's novel of the same name, which Project Gutenberg lists as public domain in the US — we did not confirm the novel's original publication year ourselves, but the listing is enough to clear this layer, and it means a creator can draw on the book's text as well as the film.

The music is the one thing to check. The Penalty is a silent film, so its images are free, but any score or recording bundled with a modern copy is a separate work with its own rights, recorded long after 1920. Trademark and any restoration were not checked and stay open on our page; we have found no confirmed commercial restoration of this title to flag either way.

A little history

Directed by Wallace Worsley for Goldwyn Pictures, the film stars Lon Chaney as Blizzard, a legless San Francisco crime boss seeking revenge on the surgeon whose error cost him his legs. Chaney wore a painful harness strapped behind his knees to simulate the amputation convincingly for the camera, a performance that helped establish him as cinema's master of physical transformation, years before his more famous roles in The Hunchback of Notre Dame and The Phantom of the Opera.
