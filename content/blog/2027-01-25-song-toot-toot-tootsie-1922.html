Is "Toot, Toot, Tootsie" public domain? Yes. The 1922 song has been free in the US since 1 January 1998 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, arrange it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The song is credited to Gus Kahn, Ernie Erdman and Dan Russo. Wikipedia states it was published in 1922 by Leo Feist, Inc. The IMSLP catalog lists the same original sheet music, published in New York by Leo Feist in 1922, crediting the same three writers, so two sources agree on the year. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1922, that is 1998, so the song has now been free to use for several years.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not free any particular performance of it. See our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights. Al Jolson's filmed performance remains a separate, protected sound recording, apart from the song, and so does any newer recording made since.

Where people know it from

Al Jolson sang it in "The Jazz Singer" (1927), one of the first major talking pictures (source).
