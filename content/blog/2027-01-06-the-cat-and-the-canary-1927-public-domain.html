Is The Cat and the Canary public domain? Yes, the 1927 photoplay is public domain in the US by term expiry. The play it is based on is free too, and so, unusually, is its original 1927 score. Check the layers below before commercial use, because most copies in circulation carry newer, protected music.

Is The Cat and the Canary public domain?

Universal Pictures published the film on September 9, 1927. The Cornell University Hirtle Chart confirms all US works published before 1931 are public domain by term expiry, and under the 95-year rule this one entered the public domain on January 1, 2023. The Internet Archive hosts it under a public domain designation: see Cornell's copyright duration guide.

What other rights apply to The Cat and the Canary?

  • Score: partly free, and worth getting right. Hugo Riesenfeld composed the original 1927 accompaniment score, and since it was also published in 1927, it is public domain by the same 95-year rule. But modern commercial releases, including the 2005 Image Entertainment DVD, add newly recorded or licensed music that is separately copyrighted. The Internet Archive's copy of the film is explicitly tagged "Silent (No Music)," confirming the raw print carries no audio of its own.
  • Story: public domain. The film is based on John Willard's play, published by Samuel French in 1921 ahead of its February 1922 Broadway premiere, long past its own US term.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. We found no confirmed restoration by a major archive or distributor that added substantial new copyrightable material; check the specific copy you use.

How was The Cat and the Canary made?

Director Paul Leni, working from John Willard's play, blended German Expressionist visual technique with an American haunted-house plot, starring Laura La Plante, Forrest Stanley, and Creighton Hale. Film historians have called it a foundation of Universal's horror output, and it set the template for the "old dark house" genre that studios kept making into the 1950s. Read more on Wikipedia.
