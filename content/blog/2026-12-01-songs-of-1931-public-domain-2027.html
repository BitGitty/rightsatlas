Which songs become public domain in 2027? On 1 January 2027, every US composition first published in 1931 finishes its 95-year copyright term. We checked our evidence file for eighteen of the best-known. None of them are free yet. Each one becomes public domain on 1 January 2027 at the latest, not a day before.

The rule

A song published in the US in 1931 gets a 95-year term. That term ends on 31 December 2026, so the song enters the public domain on 1 January 2027. It is the same math behind the class-of-1931 films people already talk about every New Year.

The songs

  • Mood Indigo (Duke Ellington, Barney Bigard, Irving Mills) — first recorded as "Dreamy Blues" in 1930, published as a song the next year.
  • As Time Goes By (Herman Hupfeld) — written for the Broadway show Everybody's Welcome, later sung in Casablanca.
  • All of Me (Seymour Simons, Gerald Marks) — best known today from Billie Holiday's 1941 recording.
  • Between the Devil and the Deep Blue Sea (Harold Arlen, Ted Koehler) — introduced at the Cotton Club in 1931.
  • Dancing in the Dark (Arthur Schwartz, Howard Dietz) — from the revue The Band Wagon, not the 1984 Springsteen song.
  • Goodnight, Sweetheart (Ray Noble, Jimmy Campbell, Reg Connelly) — a UK song that became Al Bowlly's hit.
  • Heartaches (Al Hoffman, John Klenner) — a sleeper that finally hit number one for Ted Weems in 1947, sixteen years later.
  • I Found a Million Dollar Baby (in a Five and Ten Cent Store) (Harry Warren, Mort Dixon, Billy Rose) — introduced by Fanny Brice.
  • I Surrender, Dear (Harry Barris, Gordon Clifford) — Bing Crosby's breakout recording with Gus Arnheim's orchestra.
  • Just One More Chance (Arthur Johnston, Sam Coslow) — introduced by Crosby in the film One More Chance.
  • Lady of Spain (Tolchard Evans, Erell Reaves) — a British song that became Eddie Fisher's hit two decades later.
  • Life Is Just a Bowl of Cherries (Ray Henderson, Lew Brown) — introduced by Ethel Merman in George White's Scandals of 1931.
  • Love Letters in the Sand (J. Fred Coots, Nick and Charles Kenny) — a number one hit again for Pat Boone in 1957.
  • Minnie the Moocher (Cab Calloway, Irving Mills, Clarence Gaskill) — the "hi-de-ho" song, later a Betty Boop cartoon.
  • Out of Nowhere (Johnny Green, Edward Heyman) — Bing Crosby's first solo hit.
  • When It's Sleepy Time Down South (Clarence Muse, Leon and Otis Rene) — Louis Armstrong's signature song.
  • When the Moon Comes over the Mountain (Harry Woods, Howard Johnson) — Kate Smith's theme song.
  • Where the Blue of the Night (Meets the Gold of the Day) (Roy Turk, Fred E. Ahlert, Bing Crosby) — Crosby's own radio theme song, and he is credited as a co-writer.

A free composition is not a free recording. Billie Holiday's "All of Me," Crosby's "I Surrender, Dear" and every other famous version on this list are separate, later copyrights with their own clocks.
