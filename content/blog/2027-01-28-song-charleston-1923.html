The song "Charleston" gave its name to a dance craze that swept the 1920s. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

Is "Charleston" free to use?

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1923, so it became free on 1 January 2019 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

Who wrote "Charleston"?

Wikipedia states the song, with music by James P. Johnson and lyrics by Cecil Mack, premiered in the Broadway musical Runnin' Wild on 29 October 1923. IMSLP's catalog entry lists the original publisher as T.B. Harms, New York, in 1923, agreeing with that date. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1923, that is 2019.

Can you use someone else's recording of "Charleston"?

A free song does not free every recording of it. Each recording carries its own, separate copyright on its own clock. If you need the song for a video or a new release, perform or record it yourself.

"Charleston": where do people know it from?

A 1925 recording by the Golden Gate Orchestra was inducted into the National Recording Registry (source).
