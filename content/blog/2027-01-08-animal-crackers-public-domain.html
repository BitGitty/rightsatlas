Is Animal Crackers public domain? Yes, as of this year. The Marx Brothers' second feature crossed its 95-year US copyright term on January 1, 2026, which settles a question that used to depend on an old renewal search.

Is Animal Crackers free to use?

Animal Crackers (1930). Paramount released the film in August 1930. Its 95-year US copyright term expired December 31, 2025, so the photoplay entered the public domain on January 1, 2026. The Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain lists it among the works entering the public domain this Public Domain Day. See the US Copyright Office's duration-of-copyright FAQ and the Duke CSPD Public Domain Day page.

What about the music in Animal Crackers?

  • Score: public domain. Bert Kalmar and Harry Ruby's songs, including "Hooray for Captain Spaulding," come from the 1928 Broadway production. As 1928 works, their own 95-year term expired at the end of 2023, so the music was already free before the film itself. The recorded soundtrack counts as part of the film's audiovisual work, so it otherwise shares the film's 2026 date. The film was reportedly kept out of circulation from the late 1950s until a 1974 re-release over these same music rights, which have since run out.
  • Story: public domain. The film adapts the 1928 Broadway musical Animal Crackers, book by George S. Kaufman and Morrie Ryskind, which cleared its own 95-year term at the end of 2023.
  • Trademark: undetermined, with a separate catch. No trademark is known to block reuse of the film itself. The Marx Brothers' names and likenesses are protected by right of publicity and marks held by their successors, which is a branding question separate from the film's expired copyright.
  • Restorations: partly protected. A faithful restoration adds no new copyright, but Universal's modern remasters of the old Paramount library carry a thin copyright in the new transfer. Use an archival print rather than a studio disc rip.

What's the story behind Animal Crackers?

According to Wikipedia, Animal Crackers was released August 28, 1930 by Paramount Pictures as the Marx Brothers' second feature, adapted from their 1928 Broadway musical of the same name. The story follows eccentric explorer Captain Jeffrey T. Spaulding, played by Groucho Marx, at a Long Island party where a valuable painting becomes the target of more than one theft plot.
