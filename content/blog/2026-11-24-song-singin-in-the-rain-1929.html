Most people think of Gene Kelly splashing through puddles. But the song "Singin' in the Rain" is much older than the famous film. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

Can you use "Singin' in the Rain" without permission?

The song (the music and the words) is public domain in the US. It was published in 1929, so it became free on 1 January 2025 at the latest. Anyone can now sing it, record a new version of it, or print the sheet music without permission.

What's the evidence "Singin' in the Rain" is public domain?

The music is by Nacio Herb Brown and the lyrics are by Arthur Freed. Wikipedia states it was published on 27 May 1929 by Robbins Music Corp. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1929, that is 2025.

Why isn't every version of "Singin' in the Rain" free?

The song being free does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. A modern recording of "Singin' in the Rain" stays protected, even though the song is not. If you need the song for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

The same goes for the film. The 1952 film Singin' in the Rain is a separate work with its own copyright. A free song does not make the film's footage or soundtrack free.

"Singin' in the Rain": where do people know it from?

The best-known use is the film Singin' in the Rain (1952), with Gene Kelly's dance through the rainstorm (source).
