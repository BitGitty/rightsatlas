Is Blind Husbands public domain? Yes. Erich von Stroheim's directing debut, about a predatory officer pursuing a neglected wife on an alpine vacation, is free to use in the US, though a recent restoration added one thing that isn't.

Why the film print is free

Universal released Blind Husbands on 7 December 1919. Under the law that applied then, the maximum US term for a 1919 work, an initial 28 years plus a 28-year renewal, ran out no later than 1975. All US works published through 1927 are public domain today, and this one is verified public domain (US Copyright Office Circular 15a). The original negative was later shortened by Universal and then destroyed in the 1940s, so every surviving copy today traces back to a shortened 1924 re-release print.

The other layers

  • Story: verified public domain. Von Stroheim wrote the scenario, "The Pinnacle," for this film only, and it was never published separately, so it shares the same expired term as the print.
  • Score: partially protected. The 1919 release was silent. The Austrian Film Museum's 2021 restoration added a newly commissioned score by Andreas Eduardo Frank, which is separately protected. Pre-restoration copies on the Internet Archive, uploaded in 2016, do not carry it.
  • Restorations: partially protected. The Austrian Film Museum's 2021 restoration faithfully reconstructed the original tinting and two lost intertitles and reinstated 41 shots cut in a 1924 re-release; that faithful work adds no new copyright. The new score it commissioned is protected on its own.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this. There is no obvious franchise name tied to this particular title.

A little history

Von Stroheim pitched the script to Universal's Carl Laemmle in late 1918, offering to direct for free in exchange for a $200-a-week acting salary. Shooting ran from April to June 1919 on a budget that grew from $25,000 to over $250,000 once advertising was included. On release that December, it earned more than $325,000 in its first year, far above the roughly $55,000 a typical feature made at the time (Wikipedia).
