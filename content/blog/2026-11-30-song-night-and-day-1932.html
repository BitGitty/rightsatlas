Is "Night and Day" public domain? Not yet. Cole Porter's song is still under US copyright, and it becomes free on 1 January 2028 at the latest. Here is the evidence.

The short answer

Cole Porter wrote both the music and the words. Wikipedia states the song was copyrighted on 18 November 1932, written for the musical "Gay Divorce," which opened eleven days later on 29 November 1932. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1932, that is 2028, the same year "April in Paris," published a few months earlier that year, also becomes free.

Recordings are a different right

Being free as a song will not make any particular recording free. A sound recording carries its own, separate copyright with its own clock. The 1934 film performance people remember is a specific, protected recording of a specific performance, not the song itself. Once the composition's term ends, anyone can still only perform or re-record it fresh; lifting an existing recording stays off-limits until that recording's own copyright runs out.

Where people know it from

Fred Astaire and Ginger Rogers danced to "Night and Day" in the 1934 film "The Gay Divorcee," their first romantic dance duet together on screen (source).
