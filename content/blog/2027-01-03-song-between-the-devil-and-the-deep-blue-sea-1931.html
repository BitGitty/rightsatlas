On 1 January 2027, the whole "class of 1931" finished its 95-year US copyright term, and "Between the Devil and the Deep Blue Sea" was one of eighteen standards on our list. We named it in that roundup; here is the fuller story on this one song.

The short answer

"Between the Devil and the Deep Blue Sea" is now public domain in the US. It was published in 1931, so it became free on 1 January 2027 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by Harold Arlen, with lyrics by Ted Koehler. Wikipedia states it was published in 1931 by Mills Music and introduced at Harlem's Cotton Club in March of that year. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1931 that is 2027.

Recordings are a different right

The song's own copyright has run out, but any specific recording of it is a separate, protected copyright. Cab Calloway's original 1931 recording is still covered by its own sound-recording copyright, even though the song underneath it is free. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Cab Calloway made the first recording of the song in 1931 (source), the same year Arlen and Koehler wrote it for the Cotton Club revue.
