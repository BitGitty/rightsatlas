Is The Sheik public domain? The 1921 film print is, confirmed by its publication date alone. The rest of this page is shorter than most of ours, because we have not finished verifying the other layers, and we would rather show that gap than paper over it with a confident-sounding guess. Check back, or help us close it by pointing us to a primary source.

Why the film print is free

The Sheik was published in 1921. Works published before 1923 carried a flat maximum US copyright term of 75 years, so the film print entered the public domain in the US no later than 1 January 1997. No renewal research is required to confirm that.

The other layers

  • Score: not verified. We have not yet researched what music, if any, was attached to the original release or what accompanies modern copies.
  • Story: not verified. The film is widely reported to adapt Edith Maude Hull's 1919 novel, but we have not independently confirmed that novel's own US copyright status, so we are not calling the story layer free yet.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. We have not identified any specific restoration project tied to this film.

A copy of the film is viewable at the Internet Archive. Until the remaining layers are checked, treat anything beyond the film print itself as unconfirmed, and do not assume a modern copy's music or title cards carry the same free status as the 1921 images.

A little history

Directed by George Melford and starring Rudolph Valentino and Agnes Ayres, the film was adapted by Monte M. Katterjohn from Edith Maude Hull's bestselling 1919 novel. Made by Famous Players-Lasky for under $200,000, it earned about $1.5 million, set attendance records in its opening week in New York, and ran for six months in Sydney, Australia, making Valentino an international star.
