Irving Berlin wrote "Blue Skies" as a last-minute addition to a Broadway show. Is the song free to use today? Yes. Here is what we found.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1927, so it became free on 1 January 2023 at the latest. Anyone can perform it, record it or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

Irving Berlin wrote both the music and the words. He wrote the song in late 1926, dated 16 December 1926, as a last-minute addition to the musical "Betsy," but IMSLP's catalog entry lists the sheet music as published by Irving Berlin Inc. in 1927, matching Wikipedia's text that "in 1927, the music was published." The UW-Madison Mills Music Library catalog lists the same 1927 publication date. Because it was written in 1926 but published in 1927, the later, publication year is what counts. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1927, that is 2023.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not mean every recording of it is free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. A modern recording of "Blue Skies" stays protected even though the song is not. The safe path is to perform or record the song yourself.

Where people know it from

Al Jolson performed it in "The Jazz Singer" (1927), one of the first feature films with synchronized sound (source).
