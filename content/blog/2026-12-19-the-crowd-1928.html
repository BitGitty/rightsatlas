Is The Crowd public domain? Yes, the 1928 film print is. King Vidor's MGM silent drama has been free in the US since 2024. The catch is the soundtrack: almost every copy you can find carries music that is not free at all.

Why the film print is free

The Crowd was published in the US in 1928. Under the bright-line rule, every work published in 1930 or earlier is public domain by age. Even if MGM renewed the copyright around 1956, as Cornell's Copyright Information Center notes, the maximum 95-year term (1928 plus 95) expired on 31 December 2023, so the film has been unambiguously public domain since 1 January 2024 either way.

The other layers

The story is free too. It was an original work conceived by Vidor, with scenario and titles by John V.A. Weaver and Vidor, written specifically for this production — there is no earlier novel or play being adapted, so it follows the same term as the film and has also expired.

The score is the real trap. The Crowd is a silent film, so its 1928 images and intertitles are free, but virtually every digital copy in circulation — including the TCM broadcast and the popular Internet Archive upload — carries a score composed by Carl Davis in 1981 for a TCM presentation. Davis died in 2023, and his 1981 score is protected under US copyright through at least 2076. Using one of these common copies with the sound on means pairing the free pictures with a protected recording.

We could not confirm a commercial restoration from any major archive or distributor. The main copy in circulation is described by its own uploader as sourced from a TCM standard-definition broadcast, with no restoration credit given, so that layer stays open.

A little history

James Murray stars as a Midwesterner certain he is destined for greatness in New York City, slowly worn down by the city's anonymity; Eleanor Boardman plays his wife. Vidor shot extensively on real Manhattan streets, giving the film a documentary texture unusual for MGM at the time.
