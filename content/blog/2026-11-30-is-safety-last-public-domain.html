Is Safety Last! public domain? Yes. Harold Lloyd's 1923 comedy, famous for the shot of him dangling from a clock high above a city street, has been free to use in the US since 2019. The picture is clear. The score and the modern disc editions are where it gets complicated.

Why the film print is free

Safety Last! (1923). Hal Roach Studios released it on April 1, 1923, through Pathé Exchange. Works published in the US in 1923 had a 95-year copyright term, which expired at the end of 2018. The Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain names Safety Last! among the titles that entered the US public domain on January 1, 2019. See the Duke CSPD Public Domain Day 2019 page.

The other layers

  • Story: public domain. It is an original 1923 comedy with no earlier source work, so the story shares the print's expired term.
  • Score: partly protected. The film is silent. Its images are free, but any music added to a modern copy is a separate, later work with its own rights.
  • Restorations: partly protected. The Criterion Collection released the film on DVD and Blu-ray on June 18, 2013. A faithful restoration adds no new US copyright, but a new score, new title cards or bonus features on that edition can be protected on their own.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this layer yet.

A little history

According to Wikipedia, Fred C. Newmeyer and Sam Taylor directed Safety Last! for Hal Roach Studios, with Harold Lloyd playing a store clerk who climbs the outside of a department-store building as a publicity stunt that spirals out of his control. Lloyd performed much of the climb himself despite having lost a thumb and forefinger in an earlier film accident, and the finished sequence became one of silent comedy's most famous images.
