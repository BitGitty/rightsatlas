Is The Wishing Ring public domain? Yes, this 1914 silent film is free in the US. It is also a reminder that "the film is free" and "everything about the film is free" are not the same claim.

Why is The Wishing Ring public domain?

The Wishing Ring was released on 9 November 1914 by the World Film Manufacturing Company, distributed as "A Shubert Feature." In the US, everything published in 1930 or earlier is now public domain by age, and a 1914 film is well inside that line, as confirmed by Silent Era's filmography.

Is the soundtrack of The Wishing Ring public domain too?

  • Score: partially protected. The film is silent, so the images carry no soundtrack of their own. Any music on a modern copy is a separate recording or composition that may be protected on its own terms.
  • Story: not verified. The film is based on a stage play by Owen Davis, but we could not confirm the play's publication date or its registration and renewal history, so we have not cleared this layer.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. A print survives at the Library of Congress, and a 35mm restoration was made from a British reduction print, but we could not identify who made it or whether it added a new score or titles. Treat any copy you find as carrying an unknown soundtrack and unknown restoration credits until that is confirmed.

Where does The Wishing Ring come from?

Maurice Tourneur directed The Wishing Ring, a comedy-drama starring Vivian Martin, shot at the World Film Corporation's studio in Fort Lee, New Jersey. It was adapted from a 1910 Owen Davis play that had starred Marguerite Clark on Broadway. Long considered lost, a 16mm print was found in England by film historian Kevin Brownlow, and the film was added to the National Film Registry in 2012.
