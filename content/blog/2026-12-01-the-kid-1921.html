Is The Kid public domain? Yes, the 1921 film print is. Charlie Chaplin's first feature as a director — the Tramp raising an abandoned boy, played by Jackie Coogan — is free in the US simply because of its age. Which copy you use for the music is a separate question.

Why the film print is free

The Kid was published in 1921. The maximum US copyright term for a pre-1978 work is 95 years, and every film published in 1930 or earlier has now run out that term, placing it in the public domain by age. No renewal search changes that outcome for a film this old; the age rule alone settles it, which is simpler than the check needed for films from 1931 onward, where a missing 28th-year renewal is what matters instead.

The other layers

We have not verified the score, the underlying story, any trademark, or any restoration for this film from a primary source, so those layers stay open on our page. That is a gap in our research, not a sign that the rights are unusually complicated; nobody has finished checking them yet.

One thing worth flagging while that work is pending: Chaplin re-edited and re-released The Kid in 1971, trimming the film and adding a new musical score that he composed himself for the reissue. That 1971 score is a separate, later work with its own rights; it does not share the 1921 original's public-domain status. If your copy of the film carries music, check which version you have — a silent transfer from the 1921 release, or the scored 1971 cut — before reusing the audio.

A little history

The Kid paired Chaplin's Tramp with a young abandoned boy and blended slapstick comedy with real pathos, a combination that was new for a comedy feature at the time. Chaplin wrote, directed, produced and starred in it himself, distributed through First National, and the film made a star of its six-year-old lead, Jackie Coogan, launching one of the earliest and biggest child-acting careers in Hollywood.
