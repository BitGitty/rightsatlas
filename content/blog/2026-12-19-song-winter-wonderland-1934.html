"Winter Wonderland" is a holiday-season staple, but it is not public domain yet. Here is what we found about its US copyright, and when that changes.

The short answer

The song is still protected in the US. It was written and published in 1934, so it is not yet free. It becomes public domain on 1 January 2030 at the latest. Until then, treat it as protected.

The evidence

Wikipedia states the song, with music by Felix Bernard and lyrics by Richard B. Smith, was written in 1934 and published that year by Bregman, Vocco and Conn. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1934, that is 2030.

Recordings are a different right

Even once the song itself becomes free in 2030, that will not free any particular recording of it. Recordings carry their own, separate copyright on their own clock, often a longer one. Until the song's own term ends, both the composition and every recording of it stay protected.

Where people know it from

Tony Bennett recorded it for his 1968 album "Snowfall: The Tony Bennett Christmas Album," and again as a 2014 duet with Lady Gaga (source).
