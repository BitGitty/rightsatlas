Is All Quiet on the Western Front public domain? As of January 1, 2026, yes. It is one of the most recent arrivals to the US public domain, and an unusually clean one: the print, its own soundtrack, and the novel behind it are all free. Check the layers below before commercial use; the one real trap is the restoration.

Why the film print is free

Universal released the film on April 21, 1930. Its 95-year US copyright term expired December 31, 2025, so the photoplay entered the public domain on January 1, 2026, independent of any renewal question. The US Copyright Office's duration rules and Duke Law's Center for the Study of the Public Domain both confirm 1930-class works crossed that line on Public Domain Day 2026: see the Duke CSPD page.

The other layers

  • Score: free, which is unusual for this era. The recorded music, directed by David Broekman, was published as part of the 1930 sound release itself rather than registered and renewed as a separate song score, so it shares the film's expired term.
  • Story: free, and even older. The film adapts Erich Maria Remarque's 1929 novel "Im Westen nichts Neues." A 1929 work's own 95-year US term expired at the start of 2025, a year before the film. The novel is German, so URAA restoration was theoretically possible, but URAA can only restore the same 95-year term, which had already run out, so restoration does not revive it.
  • Trademark: not verified. No active mark is known to block reuse of the 1930 photoplay; the title was reused for a 1979 TV movie and a 2022 Netflix production, but those are separate works. Confirm current marks before branded commercial use.
  • Restorations: this is the actual trap. Universal and archival institutions have produced modern restored transfers, and the 4K "Blu-ray remux" copies now circulating carry a thin derivative copyright in the remaster itself, not in the 1930 film. Pull an archival print instead of a studio disc rip.

A little history

Directed by Lewis Milestone and starring Lew Ayres and Louis Wolheim, the film used about 2,000 extras, including German Army veterans then living in Los Angeles, and was shot with dual cameras to produce both a standard sound version and an "International Sound Version" for export. It won the Academy Award for both Outstanding Production and Best Director at the 3rd Academy Awards, the first film to take both prizes. Read more on Wikipedia.
