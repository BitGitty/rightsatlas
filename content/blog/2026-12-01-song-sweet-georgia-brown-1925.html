Most people know "Sweet Georgia Brown" from basketball, not from 1925. Here is what we found about the song's US copyright.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1925, so it became free on 1 January 2021 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record it or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by Ben Bernie and Maceo Pinkard, and the lyrics are by Kenneth Casey. Wikipedia states it was published on 20 March 1925 by Jerome H. Remick & Co. IMSLP's catalog lists the same 1925 edition. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1925, that is 2021.

Recordings are a different right

The song being free does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright on its own clock. The Harlem Globetrotters' familiar whistled version is a specific recording, and like any other recording it stays protected even though the song underneath it is not. If you need the tune, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

It has been the Harlem Globetrotters' theme song since 1949 (source).
