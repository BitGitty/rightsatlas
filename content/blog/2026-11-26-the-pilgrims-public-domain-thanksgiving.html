Today is US Thanksgiving, and "The Pilgrims" is about as on-the-nose a fit as a silent film gets: it dramatizes the Separatists leaving Scrooby, their years in Leiden, the 1620 Mayflower crossing, and the landing at Plymouth Rock. Is the 1924 short public domain? Yes, in the US.

Why the film print is free

Yale University Press registered this short on 18 April 1924, under Class LU, registration LU 20081, and used it to teach schoolchildren about the Mayflower landing and the Plymouth Rock story. Nobody renewed that copyright. We searched the Catalog of Copyright Entries for 1951, 1952 and 1953, plus the cumulative 1950 to 1959 motion-pictures volume, and found no renewal entry for "The Pilgrims," "Chronicles of America," or "Yale" in any of them. US works from 1924 also crossed the term-expiry line into the public domain on 1 January 2020 regardless, so the print is verified public domain (Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain, Public Domain Day 2020). Yale never had a reason to renew: the film was made as a classroom teaching aid, not a commercial release, and the studio behind the wider "Chronicles of America" series had little incentive to keep paying for a 1920s educational short decades later.

The other layers

  • Story: verified public domain. The film adapts Charles M. Andrews's 1919 history "The Fathers of New England," which is free by age.
  • Score: partially protected. The 1924 release was silent. Any music added to a later copy, including the Periscope Film transfer on the Internet Archive, is a separate, newer work.
  • Restorations: partially protected. Periscope Film's 2020 upload carries a Creative Commons tag, but that only covers what Periscope added, such as a new score. A faithful scan of a public-domain print adds no new US copyright on its own.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this. There is no obvious franchise name at stake here, unlike some of the other films we cover.
