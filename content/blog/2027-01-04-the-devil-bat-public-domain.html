Is The Devil Bat public domain? Yes. This 1940 Bela Lugosi horror quickie, about a scientist who trains giant bats to kill using a trigger scent, fell into the public domain the ordinary way: nobody filed the renewal.

Is The Devil Bat public domain?

Films from 1940 needed a renewal filed in their 28th year, around 1967 to 1968, to keep their copyright. We searched the Catalog of Copyright Entries for both 1967 and 1968 in full text and found no renewal or registration entry for "The Devil Bat" or "Devil Bat." With no renewal on record, the film is verified public domain (Catalog of Copyright Entries, 1968, via Internet Archive). Producers Releasing Corporation, a "poverty row" studio, folded in 1947, well before that renewal ever came due, and no successor stepped in to file it.

What other rights apply to The Devil Bat?

  • Score: verified public domain. The music, credited to David Chudnow, was never separately registered, so it shares the film print's status and renewal history.
  • Story: verified public domain. George Bricker's original story was written for this production and never published on its own, so it shares the same status.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A 1990 laserdisc restoration from 35mm elements adds no new copyright, since a faithful reproduction of a public-domain image does not create a new one. The 2008 Legend Films colorized DVD does: colorization is new, protectable expression even though the black-and-white footage underneath is free. A 2013 Kino Lorber Blu-ray's commentary track is also separately protected, and ripping a copy-protected disc can raise its own DMCA concerns regardless of the film's status.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this. There is no obvious active franchise mark tied to this specific title.

How was The Devil Bat made?

The Devil Bat was Producers Releasing Corporation's first horror film. Bela Lugosi signed on 19 October 1940, and the film premiered three weeks later, on 11 November, before a wider release on 13 December. It became the studio's biggest horror box-office success, fitting PRC's reputation for making films quickly and cheaply while still delivering on the genre's basic promises (Wikipedia).
