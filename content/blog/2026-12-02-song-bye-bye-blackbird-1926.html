Peggy Lee, Miles Davis and countless jazz singers have all taken a turn at "Bye Bye Blackbird." The tune itself is older than most of its famous versions, and now it is free to use.

The short answer

"Bye Bye Blackbird" is public domain in the US. It was published in 1926, so it became free on 1 January 2022 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by Ray Henderson, with lyrics by Mort Dixon. Wikipedia states it was published in 1926 by Jerome H. Remick & Co., and the music-score library IMSLP lists the same 1926 edition. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1926 that is 2022.

Recordings are a different right

The song being free does not make every recording of it free. A sound recording carries its own copyright, on its own clock. Peggy Lee's, Miles Davis's and every other famous recording of "Bye Bye Blackbird" stay protected even though the song itself does not. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Peggy Lee sang it in the 1955 film Pete Kelly's Blues (source), one of many jazz standards built on this 1926 tune.
