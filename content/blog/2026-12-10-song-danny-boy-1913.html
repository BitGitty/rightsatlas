The tune of "Danny Boy" is an old Irish air, but the words everyone sings are much younger. Here is what we found about the copyright on those lyrics.

The short answer

"Danny Boy," as we know it today, is public domain in the US. The lyrics were published in 1913, so the song became free on 1 January 1989 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The melody is the traditional "Londonderry Air," which is far older and already free. The words sung today are by Frederic Weatherly, who fitted them to that tune in 1913. Wikipedia states the song was published in 1913 by Boosey & Co., and the music-score library IMSLP lists the same 1913 first publication. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1913 that is 1989.

Recordings are a different right

The song's copyright has expired, but a specific performance of it has its own, separate copyright. A famous recording of "Danny Boy" stays protected even though the song does not. If you need the song for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Judy Garland sang "Danny Boy" in the 1940 film Little Nellie Kelly (source), one of many famous performances of this Irish standard.
