"Mood Indigo" is now public domain: what changed on 1 January 2027 Duke Ellington's "Mood Indigo" composition is now public domain in the US. Two weeks in, here is what that actually means for the song versus its recordings. song-mood-indigo-1931.jpg | Title card: Mood Indigo (1931), song public domain status in the US

We first wrote about "Mood Indigo" before it was free. It is free now. On 1 January 2027, the composition finished its 95-year US copyright term, along with the rest of the class of 1931 songs. Here is what that means in practice.

When did "Mood Indigo" enter the public domain?

The song is public domain in the US. Mills Music published it, with Irving Mills's lyrics, in 1931, and it became free on 1 January 2027 at the latest, a date that has now arrived. Anyone can perform it, arrange it or print the sheet music without permission.

Who wrote "Mood Indigo" and when?

We confirmed the 1931 publication date directly on the original sheet music's catalog record at the UW-Madison Mills Music Library. Duke Ellington's band had first played the tune on radio in October 1930, under the title "Dreamy Blues," but the song as "Mood Indigo," with lyrics, was not published until 1931. We covered that gap between the 1930 broadcast and the 1931 publication in detail in our earlier piece on this song.

Does a modern recording of "Mood Indigo" count as public domain?

Being free as a composition does not free any particular recording. Ellington's own orchestra recorded "Mood Indigo" many times over his career, and each of those recordings is a separate copyright on its own clock, most of them still protected today. If you want to use the song in a video, the safe path is a new performance of the sheet music, not someone else's recording.

Where do people know "Mood Indigo" from?

It became Duke Ellington's signature tune, performed by his orchestra for decades (Wikipedia).
