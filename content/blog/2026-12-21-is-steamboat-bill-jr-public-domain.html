Is Steamboat Bill, Jr. public domain? Yes. Buster Keaton's cyclone comedy, including the famous stunt where a house facade falls around him and he is spared by an open window, is free to use in the US.

Why is Steamboat Bill, Jr. public domain?

Steamboat Bill, Jr. was released on 12 May 1928. In the US, everything published in 1930 or earlier is now public domain by age, so the film's 95-year maximum term has already run out. No renewal search is needed for a film this old, though the usual check for a 1928 release would fall in the 1955-1957 Catalog of Copyright Entries, which the Internet Archive has scanned and put online for anyone who wants to look for themselves.

Is the soundtrack of Steamboat Bill, Jr. public domain too?

  • Score: partially protected. The film is silent, so the images carry no original soundtrack. Most circulating copies add a later score, and that recording is a separate work that can still be protected. Use a copy with no added music, or score it yourself.
  • Story: verified public domain. The story is credited to Carl Harbaugh, with uncredited gag work by Buster Keaton, and it is not based on any earlier book or play. It was published with the film in 1928, so its term has expired along with the film's.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. The film was added to the National Film Registry in 2016, but we have not confirmed the credits of any specific modern restoration.

Where does Steamboat Bill, Jr. come from?

Charles Reisner directed Steamboat Bill, Jr., with Buster Keaton as uncredited co-director, starring alongside Ernest Torrence and Marion Byron. Production began in July 1927 in Sacramento, California, where Keaton spent over $100,000 building sets including a full pier. The cyclone sequence alone used about a third of the film's budget and six wind machines to create the stunt that made the film famous.
