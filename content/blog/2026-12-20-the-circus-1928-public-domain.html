Is The Circus public domain? Yes. Chaplin's 1928 comedy, in which the Tramp is mistaken for a thief and ends up running away with the circus, is free to use in the US, as long as you steer clear of the music he added decades later.

Is The Circus free to use?

The Circus was published in 1928. Its 95-year US copyright term ran out at the end of 2023, and Duke's Public Domain Day 2024 list names it among the films that entered the public domain on 1 January 2024, so the print is verified public domain (Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain, Public Domain Day 2024). Unlike the pre-1930 films on this site, this one needed the full 95-year clock to run out rather than a renewal check, because it falls after the simpler bright line.

What about the music in The Circus?

  • Story: verified public domain. Chaplin wrote an original screenplay, not an adaptation, so no earlier source work is in play.
  • Score: partially protected. The 1928 original was silent. Chaplin composed and recorded a new score in 1967 for a 1969 reissue, and that score is a separate, later work with its own rights.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A faithful restoration of the public-domain print adds no new copyright. Criterion's 2019 edition adds commentary by Jeffrey Vance, which is new material outside the 1928 film.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this. "The Tramp" character itself is widely discussed as a trademark concern across Chaplin's whole body of work, so treat branded use with care.

What's the story behind The Circus?

Chaplin wrote, produced, directed and starred in The Circus, which began filming in January 1926 and was delayed by a studio fire and his divorce. It premiered in New York on 6 January 1928, just as sound film was arriving. At the first Academy Awards, Chaplin received a special award for writing, acting, directing and producing the film, rather than competing in those categories (Wikipedia).
