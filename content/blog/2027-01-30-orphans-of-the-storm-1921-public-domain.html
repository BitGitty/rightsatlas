Is Orphans of the Storm public domain? Yes. The 1921 film is public domain in the US by age, and so is the 19th-century play behind it. Check the layers below before commercial use, because the music on whatever copy you find was very likely added later, and a later addition does not automatically share the film's own free status.

Is Orphans of the Storm free to use?

The film was released December 28, 1921. Duke Law's Center for the Study of the Public Domain states plainly that works published before 1923 are clearly in the US public domain, so the print is free by term expiry and the renewal question never even arises for a 1921 work. See Duke CSPD's Public Domain Day 2017 page.

What about the music in Orphans of the Storm?

  • Story: public domain. The film is based on the 1874 French play Les Deux Orphelines by Adolphe d'Ennery and Eugene Cormon, staged in the US as "The Two Orphans," decades past its own term.
  • Score: partly free, with a catch. The original 1921 theatrical score by Louis F. Gottschalk was itself published before 1923 and is free by the same rule. But a modern copy may carry a different, later accompaniment, such as a piano score made for 1970s television showings, and that one is a separate work whose status depends on when it was made.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. We could not confirm a specific restoration of this film, so treat any disc or streaming release as a separate question from the public-domain print itself.

What's the story behind Orphans of the Storm?

Directed by D. W. Griffith and released through United Artists, the film starred sisters Lillian and Dorothy Gish, in what became their last film together with Griffith. Despite his earlier successes, it underperformed commercially against The Birth of a Nation and Way Down East, though critics have since praised its "sequences of theatrical sublimity."
