Is Camille (1921) public domain? Yes, the picture has been free in the US since 1997. The rights trap on this one is entirely in the audio, because nobody has distributed a copy of this silent film without adding one.

Why the film print is free

Camille was published in the US in 1921. Under 17 U.S.C. §304, its maximum 75-year copyright term, under the 1909 Act as extended by the 1976 Act, expired in 1997, regardless of whether the film was renewed in 1948-49. The 1998 Sonny Bono Act's extensions never reached a work this old. That makes the age-based rule simple here: once the full term has run, a missed or found renewal from the 1940s no longer matters.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. As a silent film, Camille has no original synchronized soundtrack. Every commercial release we found carries a newly composed or recorded score, the Kino DVD, for example, uses a Donald Sosin score, and each is an independent, fully protected work. The bare print is free; any attached audio is not.
  • Story: verified public domain. The film adapts Alexandre Dumas fils' novel "La Dame aux Camélias," published in 1848 and staged in 1852. Its US copyright expired no later than 1880 under 19th-century law.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. Kino Lorber released a DVD with a newly commissioned score, but we have not confirmed whether the print transfer itself counts as a protectable restoration under US law. We also did not find a Criterion, Flicker Alley, MoMA or Photoplay restoration of this title. Research the specific edition before relying on one.

A little history

Ray C. Smallwood directed Camille for Nazimova Productions and Metro, starring Alla Nazimova as Marguerite Gautier opposite Rudolph Valentino as Armand, released on 26 September 1921. Natacha Rambova designed the film's Art Deco sets; she and Valentino later married. The production moved the story from Dumas's 1840s Paris to the 1920s.
