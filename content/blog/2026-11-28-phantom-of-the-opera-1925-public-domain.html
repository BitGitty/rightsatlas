Is The Phantom of the Opera public domain? For the 1925 Lon Chaney film, yes. The photoplay is public domain in the US by term expiry. Check the layers below before commercial use, because the name on the poster is not as free as the film.

What makes The Phantom of the Opera public domain?

The Phantom of the Opera was published in 1925. The 95-year US copyright term has expired for every work published through 1930, so the film print is public domain in the US by term expiry, with no renewal research needed to confirm it.

Is the music in The Phantom of the Opera also free?

  • Story: public domain. The film adapts Gaston Leroux's novel Le Fantome de l'Opera, serialized in 1909-1910 and published as a book in 1910; the first English translation appeared in 1911. Read it free on Project Gutenberg.
  • Score: not free on a modern copy. The 1925 release had no recorded soundtrack; theaters supplied live accompaniment at the time. Any score you hear today is a separate, later composition or recording, and should be treated as protected unless shown otherwise.
  • Trademark: not verified, and worth real caution. "The Phantom of the Opera" is also the brand of Andrew Lloyd Webber's stage musical and its merchandise. Showing the 1925 film under its own title is common and safe; branding or merchandise that suggests a link to the musical is the risky part.
  • Restorations: a faithful restoration of the images adds no new US copyright, but a new score, colorization, or new title cards on a modern release can carry their own protection, and ripping a copy-protected disc can raise separate DMCA problems.

Who made The Phantom of the Opera?

Directed by Rupert Julian for Universal Pictures, the film starred Lon Chaney in makeup he designed and kept secret until the premiere, on a budget of $632,357. Tensions between Chaney and Julian led to reshoots by director Edward Sedgwick that reworked the film's tone; on release in September 1925 it earned about $2 million at the box office.
