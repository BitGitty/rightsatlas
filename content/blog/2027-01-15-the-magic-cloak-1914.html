Is The Magic Cloak of Oz public domain? Yes, the 1914 film print is, simply because of its age. The Oz name itself is a different question, and we have not cleared it.

Why the film print is free

The film was published in 1914. The maximum US copyright term for a pre-1978 work is 95 years, and every film published in 1930 or earlier has now run past that term, placing it in the public domain by age. No renewal check changes that outcome for a film this old: the 28th-year renewal rule only matters for works published from 1931 onward, and this one is seventeen years earlier than that.

The other layers

The score, the underlying story, and any restoration are not verified — we found no evidence for these layers yet, so treat them as open rather than cleared either way. The one flag worth raising now, ahead of finishing that research, is trademark. "Oz" is a long-running franchise name, and this one 1914 film's copyright expiring does not extinguish any separate trademark rights that exist in Oz branding today. We have not run a marks search, so treat that layer as open, not cleared, especially for anything commercial that uses the Oz name itself, rather than just this specific film's footage.

A little history

Directed by J. Farrell MacDonald and written and produced by L. Frank Baum for his own Oz Film Manufacturing Company, the film adapts Baum's 1905 novel Queen Zixi of Ix rather than one of his more famous Oz books. Distribution troubles delayed its release until 1917, well after it was made, and it survives today only in a shortened, re-edited form. It was the studio's second production, and the last Oz film released before Baum's death in 1919.
