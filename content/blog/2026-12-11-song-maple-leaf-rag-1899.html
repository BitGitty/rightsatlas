"Maple Leaf Rag" is the piece that made Scott Joplin famous and helped launch ragtime as a national craze. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

Can you use "Maple Leaf Rag" without permission?

The song is public domain in the US. Scott Joplin published it in 1899, so it became free on 1 January 1975 at the latest. Anyone can perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

What's the evidence "Maple Leaf Rag" is public domain?

Wikipedia states the copyright was registered on 18 September 1899 and the piece was published that year by John Stillwell Stark, after Joplin signed with Stark's publishing company on 10 August 1899. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1899, that is 1975.

Why isn't every version of "Maple Leaf Rag" free?

The song being free does not make every recording free. A piano roll or a modern recording of "Maple Leaf Rag" has its own, separate copyright, running on its own clock. If you need a recording for a project, make your own.
