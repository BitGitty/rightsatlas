Christmas music: which classics are public domain in the US, and which are not Jingle Bells, Silent Night and Deck the Halls are free by age. Which Christmas songs are public domain in the US? Some of the most famous ones, yes, but only because they are much older than most people guess. Others, just as famous, will stay protected for years. Here is what we could confirm, song by song, with a source for each date.

Free by age

Jingle Bells. Copyrighted on 16 September 1857 under its original title, "The One Horse Open Sleigh" (Wikipedia). In the US, everything published in 1930 or earlier is public domain by age, so this one has been free for well over a century.

Silent Night. Composed in 1818; the English words sung today were written and published in 1859 by John Freeman Young (Wikipedia). Both the German original and the English translation are long past the 1930 cutoff.

Deck the Halls. The tune is a Welsh New Year's carol published in 1794; the English-language lyrics by Thomas Oliphant first appeared in 1862 (Wikipedia). Free by age as well.

Not public domain

White Christmas. Irving Berlin published it in 1942 (Wikipedia). Songs published between 1931 and 1963 are only free if their copyright was not renewed in the 28th year, around 1970 for this one. We have no evidence that it lapsed, and the song is still licensed today, so treat it as protected: if it was renewed, it stays protected until 1 January 2038.

Rudolph, the Red-Nosed Reindeer. Johnny Marks published it on 12 January 1949, through his own company, St. Nicholas Music (Wikipedia). The same rule applies. We have no evidence that its copyright lapsed, so treat it as protected: if renewed, it stays protected until 1 January 2045.

The recording is a separate question again

Even a free carol's famous performance — a specific Bing Crosby or Nat King Cole recording, say — can still be a protected sound recording in its own right. A public-domain song does not make any particular recording of it free. See our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights.
