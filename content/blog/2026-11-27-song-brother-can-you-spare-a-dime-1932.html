"Brother, Can You Spare a Dime?" is the anthem of the Great Depression. Is the song itself free to use yet? Not quite. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song is not public domain yet. It was published in 1932, so it is not free until 1 January 2028 at the latest. For now, treat it as protected.

The evidence

The music is by Jay Gorney and the lyrics are by E. Y. Harburg. Wikipedia states it was written for the 1932 revival of the musical revue "Americana" and published that year by Harms, Inc. Barry Popik's history of the song confirms the same 1932 Harms copyright on the original sheet music. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1932, that is 2028.

Recordings are a different right

Even once the song is free, a specific recording of it will not be. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock, separate from the song underneath it. Right now, both the song and any recording of it are protected.

Where people know it from

Bing Crosby's recording for Brunswick Records was played heavily on the radio in the weeks after the show opened, which helped popularize the song nationally (source).
