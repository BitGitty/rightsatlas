Is "Jingle Bells" public domain? Yes, and it has been for a very long time James Lord Pierpont's 1857 song has been public domain in the US since 1 January 1933 at the latest, and its lyrics never mention Christmas. song-jingle-bells-1857.jpg | Title card: Jingle Bells (1857), song public domain status in the US

"Jingle Bells" is one of the oldest and most recorded Christmas songs around, but it was first published as "The One Horse Open Sleigh", a winter sleigh-ride song. We already covered its free-by-age status in our roundup of Christmas music and the public domain; here is the fuller story of the song itself.

When did "Jingle Bells" enter the public domain?

The song is public domain in the US. James Lord Pierpont published it in 1857, so it became free on 1 January 1933 at the latest. It has been free for more than seventy years, and anyone can perform it, arrange it, or print it without permission.

Who wrote "Jingle Bells" and when?

Wikipedia states Pierpont copyrighted the song on 16 September 1857, under the original title "The One Horse Open Sleigh," published by Oliver Ditson & Co. of Boston. It was republished in 1859 under the now-familiar title "Jingle Bells." A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1857, that is 1933.

Does a modern recording of "Jingle Bells" count as public domain?

The song being free this long does not make every recording free. A modern recording of "Jingle Bells," however old-fashioned it sounds, carries its own copyright on its own clock. Use a performance you have the rights to, or record it yourself.

Where might you have heard "Jingle Bells"?

Its oddest famous use came from space: Gemini VI astronauts Tom Stafford and Wally Schirra broadcast it on 16 December 1965, using a smuggled harmonica and sleigh bells (source).
