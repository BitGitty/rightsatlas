Happy Public Domain Day. "As Time Goes By" is free today, 1 January 2027, exactly as we said it would be back in December. Now that it has actually happened, here is a part of the song's history we did not cover last time: the famous "Casablanca" recording is older than most people think.

What became free today

Herman Hupfeld wrote and published the song in 1931, for the Broadway musical "Everybody's Welcome," which opened on 31 October that year. Wikipedia confirms the date, matching our own song evidence file. Its 95-year US term ran out on 31 December 2026, so today it is public domain in the US.

The recording trick nobody notices

At the time, the song was only a modest hit, according to Wikipedia. Eleven years later, "Casablanca" (1942) made it immortal, with Dooley Wilson singing it as Sam. But Wilson never got to record it commercially for the film: a musicians' strike running from 1942 to 1944 blocked any new recordings industry-wide. So RCA Victor simply reissued its original 1931 Rudy Vallee recording, which then became a number-one hit eleven years after it first came out. Brunswick did the same with its 1931 Jacques Renard recording. The "Casablanca-era" hit everyone remembers is a decade-old record, repackaged.

Recordings stay separate

None of that changes today's news: only the 1931 composition is now free. The 1931 Vallee and Renard recordings, Dooley Wilson's on-screen performance, and every later cover are each their own, separately protected sound recordings.
