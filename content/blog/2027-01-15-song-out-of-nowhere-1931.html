Two weeks into 2027, the "class of 1931" from our New Year roundup is settled into the public domain. "Out of Nowhere" was one of the eighteen songs on that list; here is more of its story.

Can you use "Out of Nowhere" without permission?

"Out of Nowhere" is now public domain in the US. It was published in 1931, so it became free on 1 January 2027 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

What's the evidence "Out of Nowhere" is public domain?

The music is by Johnny Green, with lyrics by Edward Heyman. Wikipedia states it carries a 1931 copyright, published by Famous Music Corp. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1931 that is 2027.

Why isn't every version of "Out of Nowhere" free?

The song's own copyright has run out, but any specific recording of it is a separate, protected copyright. Bing Crosby's 1931 recording is still a protected sound recording in its own right, even though the song itself is now free. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where might you have heard "Out of Nowhere"?

Bing Crosby's 1931 recording of "Out of Nowhere" was his first hit as a solo artist, launching his career away from the Rhythm Boys (source).
