Is "St. Louis Blues" public domain? W. C. Handy's 1914 classic, free since 1990 W. C. Handy's "St. Louis Blues," published in 1914, has been public domain in the US since 1 January 1990 at the latest. song-st-louis-blues-1914.jpg | Title card: St. Louis Blues (1914), song public domain status in the US

W. C. Handy called himself the "Father of the Blues," and "St. Louis Blues" is the song most responsible for that title. It has now been free of copyright for over fifteen years.

The short answer

"St. Louis Blues" is public domain in the US. It was published in September 1914, so the song became free on 1 January 1990 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

W. C. Handy wrote the music and lyrics. Wikipedia states it was published in September 1914, with the copyright registered by Pace & Handy Music Co., and the music-score library IMSLP lists the same 1914 first publication. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1914 that is 1990.

Recordings are a different right

The song's copyright has expired, but a specific recording of it is a separate copyright with its own clock. The most celebrated recording of "St. Louis Blues" is a century old and still protected as a sound recording, even though the song is not. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Bessie Smith's 1925 recording, with Louis Armstrong on cornet, is widely considered the definitive version (source).
