Is The Big Trail public domain? Yes, as of this year. John Wayne's first starring role, an epic pioneer western shot simultaneously in widescreen and standard formats, cleared its full US copyright term on 1 January 2026.

Is The Big Trail free to use?

Fox Film Corporation published The Big Trail in 1930 and did renew its copyright under the 1909 Act. That renewal does not matter anymore: the Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act capped total US protection at 95 years from publication, and that ceiling hit on 1 January 2026. The film is now verified public domain by term, regardless of the renewal (US Copyright Act §304, Cornell LII). That makes it one of the first major Hollywood productions to enter the public domain by hitting the 95-year ceiling rather than by a missed renewal decades earlier.

What about the music in The Big Trail?

  • Score: verified public domain. This is a 1930 sound film with no separately registered score; the soundtrack was produced as part of the Fox production and shares its term.
  • Story: verified public domain. It draws on Francis Parkman Jr.'s 1849 book "The California and Oregon Trail," long free, and the 1930 screenplay was work for hire sharing the film's own expired term.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A 1980s MoMA preservation transferred the decomposing nitrate negative; that faithful work adds no new copyright. A 2012 Film Foundation-backed restoration was released commercially by Fox on Blu-ray, and that disc's own additions, plus any DMCA issue from ripping it, are a separate concern from the free underlying footage.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this. There is no obvious franchise name tied to this one title.

What's the story behind The Big Trail?

Director Raoul Walsh cast 23-year-old John Wayne in his first leading role and shot the film across several states using the experimental 70mm Grandeur widescreen process. Released in November 1930, it struggled commercially because theaters would not invest in widescreen equipment during the Depression. The box-office failure kept Wayne in low-budget pictures for most of the next decade, until Stagecoach in 1939 (Wikipedia).
