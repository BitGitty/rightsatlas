Which sound recordings become public domain on 1 January 2027? Under US law, every sound recording first published in 1926 finishes its federal copyright term that day. We did not try to confirm every 1926 release, but we did confirm two of the era's best-known recordings.

The rule

Pre-1972 sound recordings run on their own schedule, set by the Music Modernization Act of 2018 and confirmed on the US Copyright Office's Classics Protection and Access Act page: recordings first published between 1923 and 1946 get 100 years of federal protection, then enter the public domain on 1 January of the following year. A recording made in 1926 turns 100 years old in 2026, so it reaches the public domain on 1 January 2027. See our full explainer for the rest of the schedule, including recordings from other decades.

Two we confirmed

  • "Heebie Jeebies," Louis Armstrong and His Hot Five (Okeh Records). Recorded 26 February 1926 in Chicago, with Armstrong's vocal chorus; one of the earliest recorded examples of jazz scat singing (Wikipedia).
  • "Black Bottom Stomp," Jelly Roll Morton and His Red Hot Peppers (Victor Records). Recorded in Chicago on 15 September 1926 (Wikipedia).

Beyond these two, we have not gone through the full 1926 release logs of Victor, Okeh, Columbia, Brunswick and every other label of the era, so we are not publishing a complete list here. Treat this as a confirmed sample, not a catalog.

Remember: this is the recording, not the song

A recording's 1926 date has nothing to do with when the song underneath it becomes free. "Heebie Jeebies" the composition was written by Boyd Atkins on its own schedule, separate from this specific Okeh recording of it. A free recording does not free the song, and a free song does not free any particular recording — see our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights.
