"Jolly Old Saint Nicholas" is a Christmas carol with an unusually tangled history. Is it public domain? Yes, and it has been for decades. Here is what we found.

Is "Jolly Old Saint Nicholas" public domain?

The carol is public domain in the US. The combined words-and-music version sung today was first published in 1881, so it became free on 1 January 1957 at the latest. Anyone can perform, record or print it freely.

How do we know "Jolly Old Saint Nicholas" is free?

According to Wikipedia, the words come from Emily Huntington Miller's 1865 poem "Lilly's Secret," and James R. Murray's tune was published separately in 1874. The standard version that joins the words and music together was first published in the "Franklin Square Song Collection No. 1" (1881), edited by John Piersol McCaskey. Because the combined, famous version dates from 1881, that is the year we use. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1881, that is 1957, nearly fifty years ago.

Is every recording of "Jolly Old Saint Nicholas" free too?

Even a carol this old has recordings that are not free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock, separate from the song underneath it. A modern recording of this carol can still be protected, even though the carol itself is not.

"Jolly Old Saint Nicholas": where do people know it from?
