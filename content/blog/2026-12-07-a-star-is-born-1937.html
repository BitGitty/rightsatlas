Is the 1937 A Star Is Born public domain? The evidence we have points to yes, but we are telling you exactly how much of the search is finished.

Why the film print appears free

A Star Is Born was released in 1937, putting its 28th-year renewal window in 1965. We read the 1965 Catalog of Copyright Entries — the motion-picture volume covering that window — and found no renewal entry for it. We confirmed this for the July-to-December half of the listing; the January-to-June half and the separate 1964 volume have not been checked yet, so treat this as a strong result, not a finished one. The film's Internet Archive listing also carries a Creative Commons Public Domain Mark, but that is a label added by the uploader, not a legal finding, so we are not counting it as evidence.

The other layers

The score, by Max Steiner, is not verified: as a sound film its recorded track would normally share the print's status, but we have not checked the cue-sheet or publication history for the music itself. The story is not verified either. The screenplay credits Dorothy Parker, Alan Campbell and Robert Carson, from a story by William A. Wellman and Robert Carson, and the plot closely resembles RKO's 1932 film What Price Hollywood? — reportedly close enough that RKO considered legal action — but we have not checked that earlier film's copyright status.

Restorations are the clearest trap. Kino Lorber issued a Blu-ray in 2012, and Warner Archive released a 4K restoration from the original Technicolor negative in 2022 with bonus radio broadcasts and cartoons. A faithful restoration adds no new copyright to the 1937 images, but the bonus material and any new additions on those discs can be separately protected.

A little history

Directed by William A. Wellman and produced by David O. Selznick, A Star Is Born starred Janet Gaynor as an aspiring actress whose career rises as Fredric March's fading star falls. Shot in three-strip Technicolor, it became the first color film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture.
