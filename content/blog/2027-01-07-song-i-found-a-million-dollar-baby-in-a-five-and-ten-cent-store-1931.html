A week into 2027, the "class of 1931" is already in the public domain, and "I Found a Million Dollar Baby (in a Five and Ten Cent Store)" is one of the eighteen 1931 standards we already named in our New Year roundup. Here is a closer look at this one.

When did "I Found a Million Dollar Baby" enter the public domain?

"I Found a Million Dollar Baby (in a Five and Ten Cent Store)" is now public domain in the US. It was published in 1931, so it became free on 1 January 2027 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

Who wrote "I Found a Million Dollar Baby" and when?

The music is by Harry Warren, with lyrics by Mort Dixon and Billy Rose. Wikipedia states it was published in 1931 by Remick Music Corp. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1931 that is 2027.

Can you use a recording of "I Found a Million Dollar Baby"?

The song's own copyright has run out, but any specific recording of it is a separate, protected copyright. If a performer recorded this song decades after 1931, that recording still has decades left on its own clock, even though the song itself is now free. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where does "I Found a Million Dollar Baby" show up in pop culture?

Fanny Brice introduced the song in the Broadway revue Billy Rose's Crazy Quilt in 1931, the same production that gave it its first audience (source).
