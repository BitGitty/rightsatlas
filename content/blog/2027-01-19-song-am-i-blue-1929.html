"Am I Blue?" has been covered by everyone from Ethel Waters to Linda Ronstadt. The song itself has now been free of US copyright for two years.

Can you use "Am I Blue?" without permission?

"Am I Blue?" is public domain in the US. It was copyrighted in 1929, so it became free on 1 January 2025 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

What's the evidence "Am I Blue?" is public domain?

The music is by Harry Akst, with lyrics by Grant Clarke. Wikipedia states the song was copyrighted in 1929 and published by M. Witmark & Sons. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1929 that is 2025.

Why isn't every version of "Am I Blue?" free?

The song's own copyright has run out, but any specific recording of it is a separate, protected copyright. The original 1929 film performance, and every cover recorded since, carry their own sound-recording rights, even though the song itself does not. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

"Am I Blue?": where do people know it from?

Ethel Waters introduced "Am I Blue?" in the 1929 film On with the Show! (source), where it was one of the first songs written specifically for an early talking picture.
