Is The Mark of Zorro public domain? Yes, the 1920 film print is free in the US. But this is also a good example of a film where only one layer is actually settled, and the rest are open questions, not quiet "yeses".

Why the film print is free

The Mark of Zorro was published in 1920. In the US, everything published in 1930 or earlier is now public domain by age, because the maximum copyright term, even extended, has run out. That rule alone clears the print, without needing a renewal search. If you still want to see the paper trail, the normal check for a 1920 release sits in the 1947-1949 Catalog of Copyright Entries, and the Stanford Copyright Renewal Database lets you search that range yourself.

The other layers

  • Score: not verified. The film was silent on release, and we have not confirmed what is on any given modern copy.
  • Story: not verified. The film adapts Johnston McCulley's story, but we have not checked its own copyright status separately.
  • Trademark: not verified, and worth real caution. Zorro is an ongoing franchise character. This film's copyright expiring does not touch trademark rights, or the copyrights in later Zorro books, films and shows. Check marks before any branded use.
  • Restorations: not verified. We have not confirmed which restoration, if any, is on the copy you might use.

A little history

Fred Niblo directed The Mark of Zorro, starring and produced by Douglas Fairbanks, who premiered it on 27 November 1920 as the first release through United Artists, the studio he had just co-founded with Mary Pickford, Charlie Chaplin and D. W. Griffith. It was based on Johnston McCulley's 1919 serial "The Curse of Capistrano." Made for $169,187.05, it became Fairbanks' most profitable film up to that point, grossing more than $500,000.
