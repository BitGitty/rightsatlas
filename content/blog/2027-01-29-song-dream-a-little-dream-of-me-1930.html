"Dream a Little Dream of Me" was free a year early, and it was also recorded a decade early This 1930 song beat the class of 1931 into the public domain by a year. "Dream a Little Dream of Me" has been public domain in the US since 1 January 2026 at the latest, a year before the rest of the class of 1931, as we explained in December. This time, a different angle: the song had a busy life in 1931, decades before Mama Cass Elliot ever sang it.

The first recordings, within two days of each other

The song is credited to Fabian Andre and Wilbur Schwandt for the music, with lyrics by Gus Kahn. According to Wikipedia, the earliest recording was made by Ozzie Nelson and his Orchestra, with Nelson singing, on 16 February 1931 for Brunswick Records. Two days later, Wayne King and his Orchestra, with vocals by Ernie Birchill, recorded it for Victor. The song also became an early signature tune for Kate Smith.

A hit that kept coming back

Wikipedia notes that in the summer of 1950, seven different recordings of the song were in release at once, with versions by Frankie Laine, Jack Owens, and Ella Fitzgerald with Louis Armstrong among them. The song was already a repeat hit almost two decades before Mama Cass Elliot's 1968 recording with The Mamas & the Papas made it famous to a new generation.

Still free, still not the recordings

None of those recordings are free, no matter how old some of them are. The 1930 composition's own copyright ran out, but every recording of it, from Ozzie Nelson's 1931 version to Mama Cass's 1968 one, is a separate sound recording with its own clock.
