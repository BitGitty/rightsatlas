Is A Dog's Life public domain? Yes, the 1918 film is. Charlie Chaplin's copyright on it ran its full term and expired. What has not expired is the trademark in the character he plays.

Why the film print is free

A Dog's Life was published in the US in 1918. The maximum copyright term under the 1909 Act, as later extended, is 75 years for a pre-1923 work (a 28-year first term plus a 47-year renewal) — even assuming the film was renewed on schedule in 1945-46, that term ran to the end of 1993. As of 2026 the term is fully used up, following the method the US Copyright Office's own FAQ on duration for pre-1978 works sets out.

The other layers

The screenplay, written solely by Chaplin with no earlier source identified, was published as part of the film and shares its expired term — that layer is free too. The score was never an issue in 1918: it was a silent film shown with intertitles, not a synchronized recording. But any copy carrying added music, including the orchestral score substituted in for the 1959 "Chaplin Revue" re-release, carries a separately protected audio track, and a 2026 reconstruction from MoMA may carry a newly licensed accompaniment of its own.

The real trap is trademark, not copyright. Roy Export SAS and Association Chaplin actively manage trademark rights in the Little Tramp's look — the bowler hat, cane, toothbrush mustache and baggy trousers as a unified mark — and in the "Charlie Chaplin" name itself. Trademark protection does not expire the way copyright does. Using the footage in a documentary or archival project is on solid ground; branding merchandise with the Tramp's image is a different, live risk. The 2026 MoMA 4K reconstruction, made at the Cineric laboratory under Roy Export's aegis, also adds new editorial and restoration work of its own and should not be copied without a license, even though it adds no new copyright to the underlying 1918 images themselves.

A little history

Released 14 April 1918, this was the first film under Chaplin's new $1 million contract with First National. The Tramp teams up with a stray dog named Scraps, and the two outcasts find a buried wallet that changes their luck.
