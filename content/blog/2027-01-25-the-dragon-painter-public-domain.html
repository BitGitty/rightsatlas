Is The Dragon Painter public domain? Yes. This 1919 silent romance, made by and starring Sessue Hayakawa, is free to use in the US, a rare case of a landmark early film with no studio gatekeeping its images.

Why the film print is free

The film was first released on September 28, 1919, through Hayakawa's own Haworth Pictures Corporation. Works this old have long passed their maximum possible US copyright term, as the Cornell Copyright Information Center's term chart confirms, with no renewal needed to establish that. The film print is public domain.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The 1919 print has no recorded soundtrack. A 2024 Milestone Films and Kino Lorber Blu-ray adds two newly commissioned scores, by Mas Koga and Makia Matsumura, and those are separate, protected works.
  • Story: public domain. Richard Schayer's screenplay adapted Mary McNeil Fenollosa's 1906 novel of the same name, which has also passed its US term.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks tied to the Haworth Pictures name.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A 1988 preservation restoration by the American Film Institute, George Eastman House and MoMA, and a newer 2023 4K restoration by the San Francisco Silent Film Festival, Eye Filmmuseum and George Eastman Museum, add no new copyright to the images by themselves, but the 2024 Blu-ray they appear on is copy-protected and bundles the two new scores above.

A little history

William Worthington directed the film for Haworth Pictures, the company Hayakawa founded himself, with distribution by Robertson-Cole. It was adapted from Mary McNeil Fenollosa's novel and filmed on location in Yosemite Valley and at the Japanese Tea Garden in Coronado, California. Hayakawa plays an obsessed painter searching for a lost princess he believes has been transformed into a dragon, opposite his real-life wife, Tsuru Aoki, according to Wikipedia. Hayakawa founded Haworth Pictures himself and produced the film through that company.
