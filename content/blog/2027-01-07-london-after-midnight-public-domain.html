Is London After Midnight public domain? Yes, but there is almost nothing left to use. Tod Browning's 1927 mystery-horror film, starring Lon Chaney, is one of the most famous lost films in cinema.

What makes London After Midnight public domain?

MGM published the film in 1927, and Loew's Inc. renewed its copyright in the 28th year. Under the Sonny Bono Act, that gave it a 95-year US term, which ran out at the end of 2022. The Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain lists it among the works that became free on January 1, 2023. The film print is public domain.

Is the music in London After Midnight also free?

  • Score: partially protected. The 1927 original had no recorded score; theaters supplied their own live music. The only moving-image version available today, a 2002 photographic reconstruction, carries a newly recorded score, which is a separate work.
  • Story: public domain. Tod Browning wrote the original story, "The Hypnotist," directly for this film, so it carries the same 1927 status.
  • Trademark: not verified, and worth real caution. Chaney's top-hatted, sharp-toothed look in this film, often called the "Man in the Beaver Hat," is well-known horror imagery, and Warner Bros. Discovery, as the MGM catalog's successor, may claim commercial rights to it. We found no active registration, but did not run a full trademark search.
  • Restorations: partially protected. The 2002 TCM reconstruction, built from surviving stills with added camera motion, carries its own, newer copyright in that assembly, separate from the 1927 stills themselves.

Who made London After Midnight?

Tod Browning directed the film for MGM, with Lon Chaney playing both a Scotland Yard detective and a vampire-like figure used in an elaborate disguise, in a murder investigation set around a supposedly haunted London mansion. The production used live bats, armadillos and owls to build the atmosphere, according to Wikipedia. A 1965 vault fire destroyed the last known complete print.
