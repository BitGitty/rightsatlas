"Alexander's Ragtime Band" turned the whole country into ragtime fans in 1911, years before Irving Berlin wrote anything else famous. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song (the music and the words) is public domain in the US. Irving Berlin published it in 1911, so it became free on 1 January 1987 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

Wikipedia states the song was registered for copyright and published on 18 March 1911 by the Ted Snyder Company, Berlin's own publishing firm at the time. IMSLP's sheet-music catalog agrees on the 1911 publication. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1911, that is 1987.

Recordings are a different right

The song being free does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. A modern recording of "Alexander's Ragtime Band" stays protected, even though the song is not. The safe path for a video or a new release is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Many people know it from the 1938 film Alexander's Ragtime Band (source).
