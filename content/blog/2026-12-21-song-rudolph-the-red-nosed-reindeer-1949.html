We already covered "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer" briefly in our roundup of Christmas songs. Here is the fuller picture: Rudolph is really three separate works, each with its own copyright.

The short answer

The song is not public domain yet. Johnny Marks published it in 1949, so it does not become free until 1 January 2045 at the latest. Treat it as protected for a long time to come.

The evidence

Wikipedia states that Johnny Marks wrote the song, distinct from Robert L. May's original 1939 story, and published it himself on 12 January 1949, before St. Nicholas Music Publishing Co. took it up on 9 May 1949. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1949, that is 2045.

Three works, not one

Robert L. May wrote the original "Rudolph" story in 1939 for Montgomery Ward, a separate work with its own copyright history. Johnny Marks then wrote the 1949 song, the one we track here. Gene Autry's 1949 recording of that song is a third, separate copyright again, on the sound-recording clock, not the composition's. All three can reach the public domain on different dates.

Where people know it from

Gene Autry's 1949 recording hit number one on the US charts and sold 1.75 million copies that Christmas season (source).
