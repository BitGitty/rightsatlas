"All of Me" just crossed into the public domain. The song was published in 1931, and the whole class of 1931 US works finished its 95-year term on 31 December 2026. As of 1 January 2027, it is free.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1931, so it became free on 1 January 2027 at the latest, and that date has now arrived. Anyone can perform it, record it or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by Seymour Simons and the lyrics are by Gerald Marks. Wikipedia states it was written and copyrighted in 1931, published by Irving Berlin, Inc. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1931, that is 2027.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. Billie Holiday's famous 1941 recording is a separate, still-protected work, even though the song underneath it is now free.

Where people know it from

Billie Holiday's 1941 recording is the best-known version (source).
