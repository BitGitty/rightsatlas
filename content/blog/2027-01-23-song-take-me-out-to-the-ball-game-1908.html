Every seventh-inning stretch at a US ballpark echoes with "Take Me Out to the Ball Game." The song has been free to use for more than two decades now.

The short answer

"Take Me Out to the Ball Game" is public domain in the US. It was published in 1908, so the song became free on 1 January 1984 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by Albert Von Tilzer, with lyrics by Jack Norworth. Wikipedia states it was published in 1908 by the York Music Co. of New York, and a sheet-music record held by Connecticut College lists the same 1908 edition. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1908 that is 1984.

Recordings are a different right

The song's own copyright has run out, but any specific recording of it is a separate, protected copyright. A stadium organist's or orchestra's particular recording of the song can still be protected, even though the song itself is not.

Where people know it from

It is the seventh-inning-stretch anthem of American baseball, and it also gave its name to the 1949 MGM musical starring Gene Kelly and Frank Sinatra (source).
