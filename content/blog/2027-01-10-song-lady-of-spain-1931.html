Is "Lady of Spain" public domain? Yes, since 1 January 2027 The British 1931 song "Lady of Spain," later a hit for Eddie Fisher, is now public domain in the US on the same 95-year schedule as American works. song-lady-of-spain-1931.jpg | Title card: Lady of Spain (1931), song public domain status in the US

"Lady of Spain" was written in Britain, not America, but it still follows the US rules we apply to everything else. Here is what we found.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1931, so it became free on 1 January 2027 at the latest, and that date has now arrived.

The evidence

The music is by Tolchard Evans and the lyrics are by Erell Reaves. Wikipedia states it was copyrighted on 4 February 1931 in Great Britain and first published there. Foreign-published works follow the same 95-year US term, counted from first publication, so the 1931 date applies here just as it would for an American song. A work published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1931, that is 2027.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. Eddie Fisher's 1952 hit recording is a separate, still-protected work, even though the 1931 song underneath it is now free.

Where people know it from

Eddie Fisher's 1952 recording made it a hit in the US two decades after it was written (source).
