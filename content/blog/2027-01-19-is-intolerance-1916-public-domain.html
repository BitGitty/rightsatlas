Is Intolerance public domain? Yes. D. W. Griffith's 1916 epic, which tells four stories from four different eras, is free to use in the US.

Why is Intolerance public domain?

The film was published in the US on September 5, 1916. Works published before 1923 carried a flat maximum US copyright term of 75 years, so this 1916 release was already public domain by 1 January 1992 — decades before the Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain's Public Domain Day 2026, when the longer term that applies only to 1923-and-later works newly freed 1930 releases. The film print is public domain by term expiry.

Is the soundtrack of Intolerance public domain too?

  • Score: mostly free, with a catch. The original 1916 score, credited to Joseph Carl Breil and Julián Carrillo, is old enough to be free too. Newer scores, such as Carl Davis's for a 1989 restoration and Joseph Turrin's for a 2002 Kino release, are separate, newer works.
  • Story: public domain. Griffith wrote the original screenplay himself, with no earlier source work involved.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks.
  • Restorations: partially protected. Several modern restorations exist, including a 1989 Thames Silents/Photoplay version by Kevin Brownlow and David Gill with restored tints, a separate 1989 version from Gillian Anderson, MoMA and the Library of Congress, and a 2002 Kino International release. A faithful restoration adds no new copyright, but the new scores and titles added for these releases can be protected on their own.

Where does Intolerance come from?

Griffith built Intolerance as an anthology spanning roughly 2,500 years, weaving together a modern American story with ancient Babylonian, Biblical and Renaissance French narratives. The production was enormous for its time: sets that rose some 300 feet for the Babylon scenes, more than 3,000 extras, and around 300,000 feet of film shot in total. Griffith had started the modern story first, then expanded the film with the historical strands, in part as a response to criticism of his earlier film The Birth of a Nation, according to Wikipedia.
