"Happy Days Are Here Again" became a political anthem long before it became a jazz standard. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

When did "Happy Days Are Here Again" enter the public domain?

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1929, so it became free on 1 January 2025 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

Who wrote "Happy Days Are Here Again" and when?

Wikipedia describes it as a 1929 song, with music by Milton Ager and lyrics by Jack Yellen, originally published by Ager, Yellen and Bornstein. The page states its copyright was renewed in 1956, confirming the song completed the standard 95-year US term and entered the public domain on 1 January 2025. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1929, that is 2025.

Can you use a recording of "Happy Days Are Here Again"?

A free song does not free every recording of it. Each recording carries its own, separate copyright on its own clock. If you need the song for a video or a new release, perform or record it yourself.

Where do people know "Happy Days Are Here Again" from?

It became the campaign song of Franklin D. Roosevelt's 1932 presidential campaign, after first being heard in the film Chasing Rainbows (1930) (source).
