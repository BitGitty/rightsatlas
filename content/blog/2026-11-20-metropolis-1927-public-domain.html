People ask "is Metropolis public domain?" and want a yes-or-no answer. In the US, the answer is yes, since 1 January 2023. But this German science-fiction classic gives a different answer depending on which layer, and which country, you ask about.

Why the film print is free in the US

Metropolis was published in Germany in 1927. Its lapsed US copyright was restored in 1996 by the URAA, the treaty law that brought back US rights in foreign works, a fight that reached the Supreme Court in Golan v. Holder. Even with that restoration, the maximum US term is capped at 95 years from publication, and that ran out at the end of 2022. Duke Law's Public Domain Day 2023 page lists Metropolis among the films that entered the US public domain on 1 January 2023.

Outside the US, the clock runs differently. German law counts a film's term from the death of its last-surviving key creator, and director Fritz Lang died in 1976. That keeps Metropolis protected in Germany, and most of the EU, until 1 January 2047.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. Gottfried Huppertz's 1927 score is public domain as sheet music, but any modern recording of it, or a newer score such as Giorgio Moroder's 1984 version, is a separate, protected work.
  • Story: verified public domain. Thea von Harbou's 1925 source novel entered the US public domain in 2021, and the full text is free on Project Gutenberg.
  • Trademark: likely public domain. We found no blocking marks on the title.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A faithful restoration of a public-domain film adds no new US copyright. But "The Complete Metropolis" (2010), built from reels found in Buenos Aires, carries its own newly recorded score and restoration credits, and those are protected. Use a pre-2010 archival print if you want to avoid that layer.

A little history

Fritz Lang directed Metropolis for UFA, and it premiered on 10 January 1927 at the UFA-Palast am Zoo in Berlin, with Brigitte Helm, Gustav Fröhlich and Alfred Abel in the lead roles. The production took seventeen months and cost 5.3 million Reichsmarks, one of the most expensive films of the silent era.
