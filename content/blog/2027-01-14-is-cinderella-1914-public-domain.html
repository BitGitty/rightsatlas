Is the 1914 Cinderella public domain? Yes. Mary Pickford's silent version of the fairy tale was first published in the US in 1914, which puts it well past any US copyright term. The story behind it is just as free, since Charles Perrault wrote it centuries ago. The trap, as with most silent films, is in what a given copy adds on top of the original images.

Is Cinderella public domain?

Cinderella (1914). Famous Players Film Company, produced by Daniel Frohman and Adolph Zukor, first published the film in the US in 1914. Works first published in the US before 1931 are public domain by copyright expiration, regardless of notice or registration, which is a simpler test than the renewal search a 1930s or 1940s film would need. See the Cornell University Library guide to copyright term and the public domain.

What other rights apply to Cinderella?

  • Story: public domain. The film adapts Charles Perrault's centuries-old fairy tale, and the film's own 1914 screenplay and intertitles share the print's expired term, so there is no separate rights holder to clear for either layer.
  • Score: partly protected. The film is silent. Its images are free, but any music on a modern copy or release is a separate, later work with its own rights, so check what you are actually downloading before you reuse the audio.
  • Trademark: not verified. Character and franchise trademarks tied to later Cinderella adaptations, including ones from major studios, are a separate question we have not researched for this page.
  • Restorations: not verified. Alpha Video and other labels have released the film on DVD, including paired with Through the Back Door (1921), but we have not confirmed whether any release adds a new score, new title cards, or other protected material on top of the 1914 images.

How was Cinderella made?

According to Wikipedia, James Kirkwood Sr. directed this silent feature for Famous Players Film Company, which released it December 28, 1914. Mary Pickford stars as Cinderella, following Perrault's story of a mistreated housemaid, a fairy godmother, a ball, and a prince's search for his mysterious guest.
