Is Outside the Law public domain? Yes. Tod Browning's 1920 crime picture cleared its US term long ago. The surviving print itself has a history worth knowing before you use it.

Can you use Outside the Law for free?

Outside the Law (1920). Universal premiered the film in Los Angeles on December 26, 1920, with general release on January 6, 1921. Works first published in the US in 1920 entered the public domain by term expiry on January 1, 2016. See the Duke CSPD Public Domain Day 2016 page.

Does Outside the Law's score count as public domain too?

  • Story: public domain. Tod Browning and Lucien Hubbard wrote the screenplay and story, with subtitles by Gardner Bradford, specifically for the film. No outside source work was identified, so the screenplay shares the print's expired term.
  • Score: partly protected. The film is silent. A score by Dr. Edward Kilenyi is reported for a 1926 reissue, and at least one Internet Archive upload carries a different, modern score. Any such music is a separate work with its own rights.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. The surviving print is reported to be the 1926 re-release, with some of Lon Chaney's Ah Wing scenes trimmed, found as a nitrate print in a Minnesota barn in the 1970s and donated to the American Film Institute. A faithful copy adds no new US copyright, but a modern DVD or upload may add new scores or titles, and no specific modern restoration has been confirmed.

What's the history behind Outside the Law?

According to Wikipedia, Tod Browning directed this Universal crime film for Priscilla Dean, who plays Molly Madden, daughter of a reformed criminal leader, drawn back into crime after a gangster frames her father for murder. Lon Chaney plays a dual role, the villainous "Black Mike" Sylva and the gentle Ah Wing, his first Asian role and his second film with Browning. The story builds to a shootout in San Francisco's Chinatown.
