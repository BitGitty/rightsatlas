Is The Gold Rush public domain? Yes, the 1925 film print is. Charlie Chaplin's silent comedy about his Tramp character in the Klondike is free to use in the US. We checked the layers below before saying so.

Why the film print is free

Chaplin released The Gold Rush on June 26, 1925, through United Artists. US copyright on a work from that year runs for 95 years, and that term has now run out, so the 1925 print is public domain by term expiry.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The 1925 original has no soundtrack. For a 1942 reissue, Chaplin added a recorded score and his own narration, work that earned Academy Award nominations for composer Max Terr and sound recordist James L. Fields. That score and narration are a separate, newer work from the silent images.
  • Story: public domain. Chaplin wrote an original screenplay for the film, so there is no earlier source work with its own rights to worry about.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks tied to the film.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A faithful restoration, such as the 2012 Criterion Blu-ray, which pairs a 1925 reconstruction with the 1942 narrated cut, or the 2025 4K version shown at Cannes, adds no new copyright by itself, but any new score or titles bundled with it can be protected. Ripping a copy-protected disc can also raise its own legal risk under 17 U.S.C. 1201, separate from the film's public domain status.

A little history

Chaplin wrote, produced and directed The Gold Rush himself. He drew on real photographs of the Klondike Gold Rush and the story of the Donner Party, and he filmed part of it on location near Truckee, California, where about 600 extras re-enacted the climb up Chilkoot Pass, with the remaining scenes shot on studio backlots and stages at Chaplin's own Hollywood facility, according to Wikipedia.
