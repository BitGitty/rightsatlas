Is The Ace of Hearts public domain? Yes. This 1921 crime drama, starring Lon Chaney as a member of a secret society that assassinates by drawn card, is free to use in the US, a century after it first opened.

Why the film print is free

Goldwyn Pictures released the film on October 21, 1921. The US copyright term for a work from that year, 95 years from publication, has expired, as confirmed by the general US copyright term rules for works this old. No renewal check is needed to settle the question for a film this far past its term. The film print is public domain.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The film is silent, so any music on a modern copy is a separate work and a separate recording. One Internet Archive upload, titled Ace_of_Hearts_1921, was posted without music by its uploader for exactly this reason, since DVD scores are under copyright.
  • Story: public domain. Ruth Wightman's screenplay adapted Gouverneur Morris's story "The Purple Flask," serialized in two parts in Cosmopolitan magazine in 1917, which has also passed its US term.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A colorized, upscaled version titled theaceofhearts4k circulates on the Internet Archive, and the film is also on commercial DVD. A faithful transfer of the public-domain print adds no new copyright, but colorization, added music and new titles can be protected. Use an uncolorized, unscored copy to avoid that added material.

A little history

Wallace Worsley directed the film for Goldwyn Pictures. Lon Chaney, Leatrice Joy and John Bowers star in a story about a secret society that draws playing cards to choose who will carry out an assassination. Sam Goldwyn objected to the ending as written, finding it implausible, so it was reshot into the version that survives today, one that ends with the hero's sacrifice letting the two leads escape, according to Wikipedia.
