"Ol' Man River" is one of the best-known songs from the Broadway stage. Is it public domain? Yes. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1927, so it became free on 1 January 2023 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record it or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by Jerome Kern and the lyrics are by Oscar Hammerstein II. Wikipedia states the song was published in 1927 by T. B. Harms Co. and premiered with the original Broadway production of "Show Boat" on 27 December 1927. Hammerstein had drafted the lyrics as early as 1925 while the show was being written, but the song was not published until 1927, with the show, so the publication year is what counts. The official Rodgers & Hammerstein site also credits it "©1927 by Chappell & Co., Inc." A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1927, that is 2023.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not make every recording of it free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. Paul Robeson's famous film performance is a separate, still-protected recording, even though the song itself is not.

Where people know it from

Paul Robeson's performance in the 1936 film adaptation of "Show Boat" is widely considered the most famous rendition (source).
