We already flagged "Deck the Halls" as free by age in our roundup of public-domain Christmas music. Here is a closer look at where the English words actually came from.

Is the song "Deck the Halls" in the public domain?

"Deck the Halls," as English speakers sing it, is public domain in the US. The English lyrics were published in 1862, so even on the latest possible reading the song became free on 1 January 1938. In practice, everything published in 1930 or earlier is public domain by age, so this carol has been free for well over a century.

When was "Deck the Halls" published?

Wikipedia traces two separate dates. The tune is the traditional Welsh New Year's carol "Nos Galan," with the melody published in 1794. But the English words known today were written by the Scottish poet and translator Thomas Oliphant, and first published in 1862 in volume 2 of "Welsh Melodies." Because the English lyrics are the newer part, we use 1862, the later date, for the song as most people know it.

Can you use a recording of "Deck the Halls"?

The carol itself is free, but any specific recording of it is a separate copyright with its own clock. Nat King Cole's famous version of "Deck the Halls" is a protected sound recording, even though the song underneath it is not.

Where does "Deck the Halls" show up in pop culture?

Nat King Cole's recording of "Deck the Halls" reached number 16 on the US Billboard Hot 100 and was certified gold in the UK and New Zealand (source).
