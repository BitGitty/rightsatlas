Is "Lover, Come Back to Me" public domain? Yes, since 2024 The 1928 Romberg and Hammerstein song, from the musical The New Moon, has been free in the US since 1 January 2024 at the latest. song-lover-come-back-to-me-1928.jpg | Title card: Lover, Come Back to Me (1928), song public domain status in the US

"Lover, Come Back to Me" came from a 1920s Broadway operetta and went on to become a jazz standard. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1928, so it became free on 1 January 2024 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

The evidence

Wikipedia states the song, with music by Sigmund Romberg and lyrics by Oscar Hammerstein II, was published in 1928 for the Broadway musical The New Moon, where it was introduced by Evelyn Herbert and Robert Halliday. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1928, that is 2024.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not free every recording of it. Each recording carries its own, separate copyright on its own clock. If you need the song for a video or a new release, perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Many people know it from Billie Holiday's 1944 recording (source).
