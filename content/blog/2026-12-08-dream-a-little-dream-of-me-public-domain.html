Is "Dream a Little Dream of Me" public domain? Yes, since 1 January 2026 — a full year before the famous class of 1931 we write about elsewhere on this site. The reason is a single date on its copyright registration, and it is a good lesson in how these dates actually work.

Why it is already free

The song, written by Fabian Andre and Wilbur Schwandt with lyrics by Gus Kahn, is usually remembered as a 1931 song. But its earliest copyright was registered on 29 December 1930, not in 1931. Wikipedia's account of the song states this directly, and our own song page carries the same evidence. Because the 95-year US term counts from the registration date, not from the year printed on later sheet music, this one finished its term on 31 December 2025 and became public domain the next morning, 1 January 2026.

The registration-date trap

The year people casually attach to an old song — the one on a database, a reissue, or common memory — is a hint, not proof. What actually counts is the date on the copyright record. A song can be "really" a late-1930 work even though everyone calls it a 1931 song, and that one date can move its public-domain year earlier by a full twelve months. It is the same trap that caught the films Little Caesar and Cimarron, which premiered in 1931 but were registered in December 1930 and so were already free a year before the rest of their class.

What about the famous recording?

The version most people know is Mama Cass Elliot's 1968 recording with The Mamas & the Papas. That recording is a separate, much later copyright, still protected today regardless of what happens to the underlying 1930 song. A free composition does not free anyone's recording of it; see our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights for the rest of that rule.
