Happy Public Domain Day. "Love Letters in the Sand" is one of eighteen 1931 songs that just finished their 95-year US copyright term. We listed the whole class in our 1931 songs roundup; here is a closer look at this one.

Can you use "Love Letters in the Sand" without permission?

The song is now public domain in the US. It was published in 1931, so it became free on 1 January 2027. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without permission.

What's the evidence "Love Letters in the Sand" is public domain?

Wikipedia states the song, with music by J. Fred Coots and lyrics by Nick and Charles Kenny, was published by Bourne, Inc. in New York in 1931. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1931, that is 2027.

Why isn't every version of "Love Letters in the Sand" free?

The song being free does not make every recording free. A recording has its own, separate copyright on its own clock, decades younger than the song it is based on.

Where might you have heard "Love Letters in the Sand"?

It is best known today from Pat Boone's 1957 recording, which went to number one, twenty-six years after the song was written (source).
