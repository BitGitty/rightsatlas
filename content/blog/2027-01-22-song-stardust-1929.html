"Stardust" has two different years attached to it, which makes its copyright story worth untangling. Is it public domain? Yes. Here is what we found.

Can you use "Stardust" without permission?

The song is public domain in the US. It became free on 1 January 2025 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record it or print the sheet music without permission.

What's the evidence "Stardust" is public domain?

Hoagy Carmichael composed the instrumental tune in 1927, and Mills Music published it as piano sheet music on 19 January 1929. But the famous version, with Mitchell Parish's vocal lyrics, was not published until 10 May 1929, as "Star Dust" (Wikipedia). Because the famous version's lyrics came later than the tune, we use the later year, 1929. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1929, that is 2025.

Why isn't every version of "Stardust" free?

A free song does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. Artie Shaw's 1940 recording, a million-seller inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1988, is a separate, still-protected work, even though the song itself is not.
