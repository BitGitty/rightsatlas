Happy Public Domain Day. As of today, 1 January 2027, works published in the United States in 1931 have finished their 95-year copyright term. They now belong to everyone in the US: free to watch, share, adapt, remix and sell.

Films that are free today

Among them: Dracula, Frankenstein, Chaplin's City Lights, Fritz Lang's M, The Public Enemy, the 1931 Maltese Falcon and the Marx Brothers' Monkey Business. Our free dataset lists 33 films of the class: JSON and CSV, released under CC0.

Three things to check before you use one

  • Which copy are you using? The 1931 release is free. A modern restoration with new work, or a copy with a newly recorded score, may not be. An original-release print, or a copy of one, is the safe choice.
  • Trademarks. Characters that a studio still uses as brands (Universal's monsters are the obvious case) can be protected as trademarks. Using the film is one thing; selling products that look like official merchandise is another.
  • Underlying works. A film based on an older novel or play is now free as a film. But if a later work or translation it used is still protected, that later work keeps its own rights.

Foreign films too

Fritz Lang's M is German. Many foreign films had their US copyright restored in 1996, but restoration never adds time past the normal term. A 1931 film's US term still ends after 2026, so M is free in the US today as well.

Not on the list, but already free

Little Caesar and Cimarron premiered in early 1931 but were registered in December 1930. They became free a year ago, on 1 January 2026. Always check the date on the copyright record.
