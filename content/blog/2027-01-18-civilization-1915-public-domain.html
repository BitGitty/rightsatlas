Is Civilization public domain? Yes. The film was released in 1915-1916, and every US work from that period is now public domain by age. The story behind it is free too, which is a cleaner result than most films this old get. Check the layers below before commercial use, since the audio on any copy you find was very likely added separately, long after the original release.

Can you use Civilization for free?

Civilization premiered in 1916 (copies on the Internet Archive date it to April and June 1916); the queue records it by its 1915 production year. Either date falls well before 1931. Duke Law's Center for the Study of the Public Domain confirms that US works published through 1930 are now public domain by term, on its Public Domain Day 2026 page.

Does Civilization's score count as public domain too?

  • Story: public domain. According to Wikipedia, the story is an original screenplay by C. Gardner Sullivan, not an adaptation of any earlier novel or play, and it shares the film's own expired term.
  • Score: partly free, with a catch. Wikipedia credits the original 1916 score to Hugo Riesenfeld and Victor Schertzinger, and that score is public domain by the same age rule. A score on a copy you find today may instead be a newer recording or arrangement, which would be separately protected, so check each copy's credits.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified.

What's the history behind Civilization?

Produced by Thomas H. Ince and directed by Ince together with Reginald Barker and Raymond B. West, from a screenplay by C. Gardner Sullivan and Edward Sloman, the film starred Howard C. Hickman as Count Ferdinand and George Fisher as Jesus Christ. It cost roughly $100,000 to make and returned about $800,000 in profit; the Democratic National Committee credited the pacifist drama with helping re-elect Woodrow Wilson in 1916, though it was pulled from distribution after the US entered the First World War.
