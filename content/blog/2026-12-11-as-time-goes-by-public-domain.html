Is "As Time Goes By" public domain? The song becomes free in the US on 1 January 2027 at the latest. Casablanca, the 1942 film that made it immortal, is a completely separate copyright question, and this page does not try to answer that one.

The song

Herman Hupfeld wrote "As Time Goes By" for the Broadway musical Everybody's Welcome, which opened on 31 October 1931, published that year by Harms, Inc. Rudy Vallée made the first commercial recording on 25 July 1931, for Victor Records. All of this comes from Wikipedia's page on the song, matching our own song evidence file. Like every other composition first published in the US in 1931, its 95-year term ends on 31 December 2026, so it becomes public domain on 1 January 2027 at the latest.

Casablanca is a different question

The film premiered in New York on 26 November 1942, eleven years after Hupfeld wrote the song, according to Wikipedia's page on Casablanca. It is a Warner Bros. feature film from a different decade, with its own, separate copyright history. We have not researched or verified Casablanca's US copyright status, and we make no claim about it here, including about whether its copyright was ever renewed. A free 1931 song does not tell you anything about a 1942 film's rights, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

Two copyrights, same underlying idea

This split — a song on one clock, everything built on top of it on another — is the same pattern behind every old recording and every film score. See our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights for how that works in general.
