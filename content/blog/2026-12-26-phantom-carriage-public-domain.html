Is The Phantom Carriage public domain? Yes, in the US. This Swedish ghost story about a drunkard who must drive Death's carriage is older than most people assume, and its copyright term ran out years ago.

Why the film print is free

The film had its Swedish premiere on 1 January 1921 and a US release in February 1922. A 75-year US copyright term from the 1921 publication date expired on 1 January 1997. Even if the 1996 URAA restoration applied to this Swedish film, the restored term is capped at the same point: 75 years from 1921. Either way, the print is verified public domain in the US as of 2026 (17 U.S.C. §104A). This is the same URAA math that matters for many foreign silents: a restoration can never outlast the term the work would have had if it had been a US film all along.

The other layers

  • Story: verified public domain. The film is based on Selma Lagerlöf's 1912 novel "Körkarlen" (Thy Soul Shall Bear Witness), free by term since 1988.
  • Score: partially protected. The film is silent. Elena Kats-Chernin, Matti Bye and KTL each scored separate later restorations, and Criterion's 2011 US release carries two of those scores. Each is independently protected.
  • Restorations: partially protected. Several restorations exist, including a 1995 color-tinted version and a 2015 digital transfer by the Swedish Film Institute. The added scores, tinting and any new intertitles on these versions can be protected; a raw silent print carries no such extra rights.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this. The Swedish title "Körkarlen" and the English "The Phantom Carriage" both circulate; neither is a confirmed trademark issue here.

A little history

Victor Sjöström directed and starred, adapting Selma Lagerlöf's novel in a script he wrote in eight days and personally brought to her home in April 1920. Filming ran from May to July 1920 at the newly built Filmstaden studios in Solna. The film is known for its double-exposure ghost effects, shot in-camera by cinematographer Julius Jaenzon (Wikipedia).
