The Internet Archive's 2027 Public Domain Film Contest asks for a short film, up to 4 minutes, that uses at least one work published in 1931. Entries close at 11:59 pm PST on 7 January 2027. We track the 1931 film class closely, so here is what to watch for before you start cutting.

1931 films you can draw from

US works published in 1931 enter the public domain on 1 January 2027. Our free list covers 33 films of that year, including Dracula, Frankenstein, City Lights, M, The Public Enemy, the 1931 Maltese Falcon, Monkey Business and The Front Page. Download it as JSON or CSV (CC0), or browse the Class of 1931 page.

One date trap: Little Caesar and Cimarron premiered in 1931 but were registered in December 1930, so they have been free since 1 January 2026. They are free to use, but they are not "1931 works" for the contest rule. Check the copyright date, not the poster.

The traps to avoid

  • Use the 1931 version, not a restoration. A modern restoration can add new material, and many copies online have a newly recorded score. Choose a copy of the original release.
  • Trademarks. Dracula and Frankenstein are flagged in our data: Universal still uses its monsters as brands. Using footage in a creative short is one thing. Making your film look like an official Universal product, or using its logos, is another.
  • Foreign films. M (Germany) and The Speckled Band (UK) had their US copyrights restored in 1996. Restoration does not extend the normal term, so they still become free in the US on 1 January 2027, the same as American films of 1931.
  • US only. Public domain status differs by country. Our research covers the US, which is what the contest rules use.

Sound: the part people get wrong

  • The soundtrack of a 1931 film is legally part of the film, so it becomes free with it. A new score someone added later does not.
  • Separate sound recordings follow a different rule: those published in 1926 enter the public domain in 2027, not 1931 ones. The contest blog makes the same point.
  • The safest options are your own voice, your own performance of a public domain composition, or CC0 audio.

Before you upload

Keep a note of where each clip came from (the archive.org item and the film's year). If anyone ever questions it, that record answers in seconds. For every film we cover, our pages list the evidence layer by layer: browse the films, or read how we check each film.

Good luck with your entry. This is research, not legal advice.
