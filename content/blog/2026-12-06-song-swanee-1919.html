Before "Rhapsody in Blue," George Gershwin's first real hit was a peppy 1919 song about the Swanee River. It has now been free of copyright for over a decade.

The short answer

"Swanee" is public domain in the US. It was published in 1919, so it became free on 1 January 2015 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by George Gershwin, with lyrics by Irving Caesar. Wikipedia states it was published on 31 October 1919 by T. B. Harms & Francis, Day & Hunter, and the music-score library IMSLP lists the same 1919 Harms first edition. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1919 that is 2015.

Recordings are a different right

The song's copyright has run out, but a specific recording of it has its own, separate copyright. The most famous recording of "Swanee" is nearly as old as the song, but it is still a different right with its own clock. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself.

Where people know it from

Al Jolson's 1920 recording made "Swanee" a huge hit and is still the version most people think of (source).
