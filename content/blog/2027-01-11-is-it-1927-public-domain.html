Is It public domain? Yes. The 1927 Paramount comedy that made Clara Bow the "It Girl" is free to use in the US. A new restoration due at a 2027 film festival does not change that for the original picture.

Why the film print is free

It premiered in New York on 5 February 1927. Duke Law's Public Domain Day 2023 page confirms that works from 1927 entered the US public domain on 1 January 2023, by age, so no renewal search is needed for this film.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The film is silent, so the images are free on their own. The San Francisco Silent Film Festival lists a new restoration with music by the Mont Alto Motion Picture Orchestra, planned for its March 2027 festival. That score, once recorded, will be its own protected work.
  • Story: not verified. Elinor Glyn is credited with the original screen story, with Hope Loring and Louis D. Lighton writing the scenario. We could not confirm when Glyn's underlying story or novella was first published, so we are not calling this layer clear.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: partially protected. The 2027 restoration, a partnership between Film Preserve Ltd., Maltese Film Works, Paramount Pictures and the festival, has not yet premiered. A faithful restoration adds no new copyright to the film itself, but its new score and any new titles will be protected once released.

A little history

Clarence G. Badger directed It, starring Clara Bow as a shopgirl who sets her sights on her boss, played by Antonio Moreno. It premiered in Los Angeles on 14 January 1927 before going into general release on 19 February. Paramount paid Elinor Glyn $50,000 for her "it" concept and gave her a role playing herself in the film, and Bow's performance made her a star and popularized the phrase "it girl."
