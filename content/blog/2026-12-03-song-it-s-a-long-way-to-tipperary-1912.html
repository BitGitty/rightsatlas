"It's a Long Way to Tipperary" started as a music-hall song, then became the marching song of a generation. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

When did "It's a Long Way to Tipperary" enter the public domain?

The song is public domain in the US. Jack Judge and Harry Williams published it in 1912, so it became free on 1 January 1988 at the latest. Anyone can now perform, record or print it without permission.

Who wrote "It's a Long Way to Tipperary" and when?

Wikipedia states the song was published by Bert Feldman in London in October 1912. IMSLP lists first editions from the same year, agreeing with that date. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1912, that is 1988.

Can you use a recording of "It's a Long Way to Tipperary"?

A free song does not free every recording of it. Recordings have their own, separate copyright with their own clock. A modern recording of "Tipperary" stays protected even though the 1912 song underneath it is not. Record or perform it yourself if you need it for a project.

Where do people know "It's a Long Way to Tipperary" from?

The song became famous when British soldiers sang it marching to war in 1914 (source).
