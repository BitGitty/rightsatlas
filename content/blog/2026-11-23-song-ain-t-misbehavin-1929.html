Is "Ain't Misbehavin'" public domain? Yes, since 2025 The 1929 song by Fats Waller, Harry Brooks and Andy Razaf has been free to use in the US since 1 January 2025 at the latest, but recordings of it are a separate right. song-ain-t-misbehavin-1929.jpg | Title card: Ain't Misbehavin' (1929), song public domain status in the US

"Ain't Misbehavin'" is one of the best-known songs of the jazz age. If you want to sing it, arrange it or print the sheet music, here is what we found about its US copyright.

The short answer

The song (the music and the words) is public domain in the US. It was published in 1929. In the US, a song published in 1929 became free on 1 January 2025 at the latest. So today anyone can perform it, record a new version, or publish the sheet music without asking permission.

The evidence

The music is by Thomas "Fats" Waller and Harry Brooks, and the lyrics are by Andy Razaf. Wikipedia describes it as a 1929 song, published by Mills Music. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1929, that is 2025.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not mean every recording of it is free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. If you take a modern recording of "Ain't Misbehavin'" and put it in your video, that recording is still protected, even though the song is not. The safe path is to perform or record the song yourself, from the sheet music.

Also be careful with new arrangements. A modern arrangement or a new set of lyrics written later can have its own, newer copyright. The 1929 song is free; someone's 2010 arrangement of it may not be.

Where people know it from

Many people know the song from the film Stormy Weather (1943), where Fats Waller himself performs it (source).
