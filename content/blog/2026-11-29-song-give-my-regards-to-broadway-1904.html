George M. Cohan wrote "Give My Regards to Broadway" for his own Broadway debut as a composer. It is one of the oldest songs we have checked. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

When did "Give My Regards to Broadway" enter the public domain?

The song is public domain in the US. Cohan published it in 1904, so it became free on 1 January 1980 at the latest. Anyone can perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

Who wrote "Give My Regards to Broadway" and when?

Wikipedia says Cohan wrote and published the song in 1904 through F. A. Mills, introducing it in his musical Little Johnny Jones the same year. A second source, Barry Popik's research blog, quotes the original sheet music cover crediting "F.A. Mills (NY), 1904," confirming the date. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1904, that is 1980.

Can you use a recording of "Give My Regards to Broadway"?

A free song is not a free recording. Every recording has its own copyright, running on its own clock, separate from the song underneath it. If you need the song for a video, perform or record it yourself rather than lifting someone else's recording.

Where does "Give My Regards to Broadway" show up in pop culture?

Most people know it from Yankee Doodle Dandy (1942), where James Cagney won an Oscar playing Cohan and performing the song (source).
