Is The Navigator public domain? Yes, the 1924 print is verified free in the US by term expiry. Not every layer of this one is settled yet, and we would rather say so plainly than guess, because a site that only shows the easy answers is not actually useful to a creator deciding what to risk. Check the breakdown below before commercial use.

Why the film print is free

The film was published September 28, 1924, according to the Internet Archive's record of it. Works from 1924 entered the US public domain on January 1, 2020, and Duke Law's Center for the Study of the Public Domain lists Buster Keaton's The Navigator among them on its Public Domain Day 2020 page.

The other layers

  • Score: not free. The film is silent, so the 1924 images carry no recorded soundtrack. Any music on a copy you find is a separate, later recording, and its status depends on that specific copy.
  • Story: not verified. The Internet Archive's record names no source novel or play, and we did not confirm one elsewhere. We are leaving this layer honestly undetermined rather than guessing at a source.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: not verified. We have not confirmed any specific restoration of this film.

The Internet Archive copy we found carries Portuguese subtitles added later, which is a reminder to check what a specific upload has added before you reuse it. A different upload with English intertitles and no added subtitles may still carry its own music track, so look at the file itself rather than assuming from the listing.

A little history

Keaton directed and starred, with Donald Crisp co-directing and Clyde Bruckman among the writers. The production chartered the decommissioned Army transport USAT Buford for $25,000 and renamed it the Navigator for ten weeks of filming off Catalina Island. It became Keaton's biggest commercial success, grossing $680,406 on a $385,000 budget, and it was added to the National Film Registry in 2018. Read more on Wikipedia.
