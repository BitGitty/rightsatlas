Is "Stormy Weather" public domain? Not yet, it frees in 2029 The 1933 song by Harold Arlen and Ted Koehler, made famous by Lena Horne, is still under US copyright until 1 January 2029 at the latest. song-stormy-weather-1933.jpg | Title card: Stormy Weather (1933), song public domain status in the US

"Stormy Weather" is a jazz standard forever linked to Lena Horne. Is the song public domain yet? Not yet. Here is what we found.

The short answer

The song is not public domain yet. It was published in 1933, so it does not become free until 1 January 2029 at the latest. Treat it as protected for now.

The evidence

The music is by Harold Arlen and the lyrics are by Ted Koehler. Wikipedia states it was written and published in 1933 by Mills Music. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1933, that is 2029.

Recordings are a different right

Even after the song itself is free, specific recordings will not be. A sound recording has its own copyright on its own clock. Lena Horne's 1941 recording, inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2000, is one example of a separately protected performance, on top of the still-protected song.

Where people know it from

Lena Horne performed it in the 1943 film "Stormy Weather," and her 1941 recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2000 (source). This post is about the song, not any particular film of the same name.
