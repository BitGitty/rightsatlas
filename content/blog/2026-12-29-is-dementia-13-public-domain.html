Is Dementia 13 public domain? Yes. Francis Ford Coppola's first mainstream feature, a low-budget horror film shot in Ireland, is free to use in the US.

Is Dementia 13 public domain?

We found no entry for Dementia 13 in the Library of Congress's Catalog of Copyright Entries for motion pictures, 1960 to 1969, the volume that indexes every film registered that decade. Its distributor, Roger Corman's The Filmgroup, is documented to have routinely skipped copyright registration, and the company closed in 1968 with no renewal on file. Long-running, unrestricted copies on the Internet Archive are consistent with this. The film print is public domain.

What other rights apply to Dementia 13?

  • Score: public domain. Ronald Stein wrote it for hire for The Filmgroup, and as part of an unregistered sound film it shares the print's status.
  • Story: public domain. Coppola wrote the screenplay directly for this production, with no earlier source work involved.
  • Trademark: not verified. The Filmgroup is long dissolved, and we know of no active franchise or licensing program for this title, though a 2017 remake exists as its own, separate work.
  • Restorations: partially protected. American Zoetrope's 2021 Director's Cut, a restoration completed under Coppola's own supervision with sound work by Earwax Productions, is a new, fully protected master released to Blu-ray and 4K. Older archival prints, including the ones circulating on the Internet Archive, are not affected by that newer copyright.

How was Dementia 13 made?

Coppola wrote the screenplay in three days. Roger Corman gave him the chance to direct, using money left over from an earlier production and shooting around Ireland's Ardmore Studios and Howth Castle. The story follows a scheming widow trying to manipulate her wealthy mother-in-law with faked supernatural events, before a string of real murders tied to an old family tragedy, involving a drowned daughter, takes over, according to Wikipedia. American International Pictures released it in US theaters on September 25, 1963.
