Is The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1923) public domain? Yes, the Lon Chaney version is free to use in the US. Not the 1939 Charles Laughton version, not the 1996 Disney film, just this one. The catch is not the picture. It is the sound on every disc you can actually buy.

Is The Hunchback of Notre Dame public domain?

Universal Pictures published the film in 1923. All US works from that year crossed the 95-year term-expiry line and entered the public domain on 1 January 2019, so the print is verified public domain (Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain, Public Domain Day 2019). No renewal search is needed for a film this old; the 1930-or-earlier bright line settles it on its own.

What other rights apply to The Hunchback of Notre Dame?

  • Story: verified public domain. The film adapts Victor Hugo's "Notre-Dame de Paris," published in 1831 and free for well over a century.
  • Score: partially protected. The 1923 release was silent. The 2021 Universal/Kino Lorber 4K restoration carries a new score by Nora Kroll-Rosenbaum and Laura Karpman, and an earlier Flicker Alley release used a different commissioned score. Both are separately copyrighted recordings.
  • Restorations: partially protected. A faithful restoration of the public-domain footage adds no new copyright by itself. The new score and any restored tinted sequences on the 2021 Blu-ray carry their own rights, and ripping a copy-protected disc raises a separate DMCA concern.
  • Trademark: not verified. Quasimodo and Esmeralda trace to Hugo's public-domain novel, but Universal and Disney both have franchise interests in the characters. We did not run a trademark search, so treat any franchise-adjacent branding with caution.

How was The Hunchback of Notre Dame made?

Universal built the film around Lon Chaney's performance as Quasimodo, for which Chaney designed his own elaborate makeup. Directed by Wallace Worsley and produced under Carl Laemmle and Irving Thalberg as the studio's 1923 prestige "Super Jewel," it became Universal's most successful silent film (Wikipedia).
