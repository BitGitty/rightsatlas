Is "Minnie the Moocher" public domain? Yes, as of 1 January 2027. Cab Calloway's "hi-de-ho" song just finished its 95-year US copyright term, along with the rest of the class of 1931.

The evidence

The song is credited to Cab Calloway, Irving Mills and Clarence Gaskill. Wikipedia states it was recorded on 3 March 1931 and published that year by Gotham Music Service in New York. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1931, that is 2027, so the song is now free since 1 January 2027 at the latest, right alongside the rest of the US class of 1931.

Recordings are a different right

A free song does not free any particular recording of it. See our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights. Calloway's original 1931 recording, and his later performance in "The Blues Brothers" (1980), stay separate, protected sound recordings on their own clocks, each counted from its own date, not from 1931.

Where people know it from

The song became the subject of the 1932 Betty Boop cartoon "Minnie the Moocher," and decades later Cab Calloway performed it again on screen in "The Blues Brothers" (1980) (source).
