Is The Freshman public domain? Yes, Harold Lloyd's football comedy has been free in the US since the start of 2021. Even its original score is free; just not every score attached to it today.

Is The Freshman free to use?

The Freshman premiered on 20 September 1925. Its 95-year US copyright term ran out at the end of 2020, so the film entered the public domain on 1 January 2021. Duke Law's Public Domain Day 2021 page lists The Freshman among that year's films.

What about the music in The Freshman?

  • Score: partially protected. The original 1925 score, credited to Harold Berg, is public domain by the same term as the film. But the widely sold 2014 Criterion release carries a new score by Carl Davis, a separate, protected work. Check which score your copy has before reusing the audio.
  • Story: verified public domain. The screenplay, credited to John Grey, Sam Taylor, Tim Whelan and Ted Wilde, was published with the 1925 film and expired with it. H. C. Witwer later said it drew on his 1915 short story "The Emancipation of Rodney," which has also expired.
  • Trademark: not verified.
  • Restorations: partially protected. Criterion's 2014 Blu-ray and DVD use a 1998 UCLA restoration with the new Carl Davis score and extras. A faithful transfer of a public-domain film adds no new copyright, but the new score, commentary and extras are protected, and ripping the disc may also raise a DMCA issue.

What's the story behind The Freshman?

Fred C. Newmeyer and Sam Taylor directed The Freshman, starring Harold Lloyd as an eager college newcomer opposite Jobyna Ralston, and it opened on 20 September 1925 at the B.S. Moss Colony Theater on Broadway. The train-station scenes were shot at a Southern Pacific depot in Ontario, California, and the football scenes were filmed at the Rose Bowl and during the November 1924 "Big Game" between UC Berkeley and Stanford.
