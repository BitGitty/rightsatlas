Is "White Christmas" public domain? Not yet, and here is why Irving Berlin's 1942 song is still protected in the US. We already flagged "White Christmas" as still protected in our roundup of Christmas music and the public domain. Here is the fuller picture: the song's own date, and the much bigger recording sitting on top of it.

The short answer

The song is still protected in the US. Irving Berlin published it in 1942, so it is not yet free. It becomes public domain on 1 January 2038 at the latest. Until then, treat it as protected.

The evidence

Wikipedia states the song was published in 1942 by Irving Berlin Music Corporation, Berlin's own publishing company. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1942, that is 2038.

Recordings are a different, bigger right here

Most people's idea of "White Christmas" is really a specific recording, not just the sheet music. A recording has its own, separate copyright, on its own clock. Even after the song's own term ends in 2038, a famous recording of it will not automatically be free with it.

Where people know it from

The famous use is Bing Crosby's 1942 recording, which became the best-selling single in history, with estimated sales of more than 50 million copies (source).
