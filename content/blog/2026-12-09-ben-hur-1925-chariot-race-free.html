Is Ben-Hur (1925) public domain? The picture is, since 1 January 2021. The chariot race, the images, all of it, is free to use in the US. What is not free is the score on the version most people have actually seen.

Why the film print is free

Loew's Inc. (MGM) published Ben-Hur in the US in 1925. Its maximum copyright term, 95 years under the Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act, expired on 1 January 2021, regardless of whether it was renewed in 1952-53. Duke Law's Public Domain Day 2021 page lists Ben-Hur among that year's films.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The 1925 release was silent and carried no synchronized soundtrack, so the print itself has no music rights. But the restoration most people know, with an orchestral score composed by Carl Davis in 1987 for a Kevin Brownlow and David Gill restoration, is a separately copyrighted score and a separately protected recording. Warner Home Video released it in 2005 as part of a Ben-Hur (1959) anniversary set.
  • Story: verified public domain. The film is based on Lew Wallace's 1880 novel, long out of copyright before the film existed. The 1925 screenplay is part of the film's own expired copyright.
  • Trademark: not verified. MGM remade Ben-Hur in 1959 and again in 2016, which points to active commercial use of the title, but we have not searched trademark registrations.
  • Restorations: partially protected, for the same reason as the score. The Brownlow and Gill restoration and the Davis score are separate, protected additions on top of a free print.

A little history

Fred Niblo directed Ben-Hur, starring Ramon Novarro, and it premiered in New York on 30 December 1925. The production had already replaced its original lead, George Walsh, with Novarro while filming in Italy, and it became the most expensive film of the silent era, at roughly $3.9 million. For the chariot race alone, the crew shot 200,000 feet of film and edited it down to 750 feet.
