Is Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans public domain? Yes. F. W. Murnau's 1927 silent drama, often named among the greatest films ever made, is free to use in the US, print and period score alike.

Why the film print is free

Sunrise was released on November 4, 1927. The US copyright term for a 1927 work is 95 years, and that term has expired, so the film print is public domain, as confirmed by the Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain in its list of works that entered the public domain in 2023.

The other layers

  • Score: mostly free, with a catch. The 1927 Fox Movietone synchronized score, credited to Hugo Riesenfeld, was published with the film and shares its expired term. A later, commissioned score, such as Joe Kraemer's 2016 replacement, or any re-recorded version, is a separate work and is not automatically free.
  • Story: public domain. The film adapts Hermann Sudermann's short story "The Excursion to Tilsit," published in a 1917 collection, which has also passed its US term and carries no separate claim on the film.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks.
  • Restorations: partially protected. Home-video editions, including a Fox Home Entertainment Blu-ray and a Masters of Cinema release in the UK, are built on archival restorations. A faithful restoration adds no new copyright by itself, but new or translated titles and extras bundled onto a disc can be protected on their own.

A little history

Murnau, already known for German Expressionist films, made Sunrise as his American debut for the Fox Film Corporation, starring George O'Brien, Janet Gaynor and Margaret Livingston. The story follows a farmer tempted by a visitor from the city to murder his wife, and Murnau used Fox's new Movietone sound-on-film system for the score and effects, along with striking tracking shots and forced-perspective effects to build its sets, according to Wikipedia.
