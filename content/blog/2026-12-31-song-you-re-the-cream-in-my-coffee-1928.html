"You're the Cream in My Coffee" is one of many hit songs from the DeSylva, Brown and Henderson songwriting team. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

Can you use "You're the Cream in My Coffee" without permission?

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1928, so it became free on 1 January 2024 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

What's the evidence "You're the Cream in My Coffee" is public domain?

Wikipedia states the song, with music by Ray Henderson and lyrics by B.G. DeSylva and Lew Brown, was published in 1928 for the Broadway musical Hold Everything!, where it was introduced by Ona Munson and Jack Whiting. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1928, that is 2024.

Why isn't every version of "You're the Cream in My Coffee" free?

A free song does not free every recording of it. Each recording carries its own, separate copyright on its own clock. If you need the song for a video or a new release, perform or record it yourself.

Where do people know "You're the Cream in My Coffee" from?

It was performed by Georges Carpentier and chorus in the 1930 Warner Bros. film adaptation of Hold Everything! (source).
