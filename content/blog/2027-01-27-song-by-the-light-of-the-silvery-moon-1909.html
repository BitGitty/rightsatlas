Decades before Doris Day borrowed its title for a 1953 musical, "By the Light of the Silvery Moon" was already a Tin Pan Alley hit. The song has been free of copyright for twenty years.

Is the song "By the Light of the Silvery Moon" in the public domain?

"By the Light of the Silvery Moon" is public domain in the US. It was published in 1909, so the song became free on 1 January 1985 at the latest. Anyone can now record it, perform it, or print the sheet music without permission.

When was "By the Light of the Silvery Moon" published?

The music is by Gus Edwards, with lyrics by Edward Madden. Wikipedia states it was published in 1909 by Jerome H. Remick & Co., and the music-score library IMSLP lists three 1909 first-edition printings, confirming the year. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest; for 1909 that is 1985.

Can you use a recording of "By the Light of the Silvery Moon"?

The song's own copyright has run out, but any specific recording of it is a separate, protected copyright. If you need the tune for a video, the safe path is to perform or record it yourself, rather than relying on someone else's recording.

"By the Light of the Silvery Moon": where do people know it from?

The 1953 film By the Light of the Silvery Moon, starring Doris Day, was named after the song (source).
