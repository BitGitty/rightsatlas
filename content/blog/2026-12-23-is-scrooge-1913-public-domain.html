With Christmas two days away, here is the oldest screen version of A Christmas Carol we could verify as free: a 1913 British short called simply Scrooge. Is it public domain? Yes, the print is, more than a century before anyone else's Christmas Carol adaptation reaches that point.

Why the film print is free

This Scrooge was released in September 1913. Works from 1928 entered the US public domain on January 1, 2024, according to the Duke Center for the Study of the Public Domain, and a film from 1913 is well past that point. Being British does not change this: by 1996, when the URAA could have restored some foreign films, a 1913 work had no copyright left in the UK or the US to restore. That second point rests on general reasoning about the dates, not on a UK-law source we checked ourselves. The film print is public domain in the US.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The film is silent, so any music on a modern upload is a separate recording, not covered by the film's own status.
  • Story: public domain. It is based on Charles Dickens's 1843 novella, which is far older than any US copyright term still running.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks tied to the production company or title.
  • Restorations: not verified. We could not confirm an Internet Archive copy of this specific film during our research, so source a print from an archive or distributor and check what it adds before you reuse it.

A little history

Zenith Film Company produced this Scrooge, directed by Leedham Bantock and starring Seymour Hicks, who had already played the role on stage since 1901. Some prints had color toning applied to the black-and-white image, and Hicks later reprised the part in a 1935 sound version, according to Wikipedia, which also notes the film was released in the US in 1926 under the title Old Scrooge. Search under both titles if you go looking for a print.
