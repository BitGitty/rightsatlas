Is White Zombie public domain? Yes. The 1932 film that invented the zombie picture was never renewed, and we checked the actual renewal catalogs ourselves to confirm it.

What makes White Zombie public domain?

White Zombie (1932). Halperin Productions released the film on July 28, 1932, through United Artists. Under the law of the time, a film from 1932 had an initial 28-year copyright term, with renewal due by 1960. We searched the full text of the Catalog of Copyright Entries for 1959 and 1960, the two volumes covering this film's renewal window, and found no entry for White Zombie or Halperin Productions in either one. See the 1959 Catalog of Copyright Entries and the 1960 volume.

Is the music in White Zombie also free?

  • Score: public domain. The recorded 1932 soundtrack, a compiled score supervised by Abe Meyer with original cues by Guy Bevier Williams and Xavier Cugat, is part of the original work and shares its public-domain status. The same 1959-1960 catalog searches found no separate score registration either.
  • Story: public domain. Garnett Weston's screenplay was written for the film and was not separately registered or renewed. The film also draws on William Seabrook's 1929 book The Magic Island, which is itself public domain by age.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked this layer.
  • Restorations: not verified. A Blu-ray rip is known to circulate, but we could not confirm its distributor or what it adds. Any new elements a commercial disc adds, such as a new audio mix, are protected on their own regardless of the film's status, and ripping a copy-protected disc carries separate DMCA risk.

Who made White Zombie?

According to Wikipedia, Victor Halperin directed White Zombie, independently produced by his brother Edward and distributed by United Artists. Bela Lugosi plays the voodoo master "Murder" Legendre, who turns a young American woman into a zombie after she arrives in Haiti with her fiancé. The film is widely recognized as the first feature-length zombie movie.
