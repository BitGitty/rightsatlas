Tomorrow, the films of 1931 enter the US public domain: what changes and what doesn't At midnight, US works published in 1931 finish their 95-year copyright term. Here is what becomes free on 1 January 2027, and the layers that stay protected. class-of-1931.jpg | RightsAtlas title card: The Class of 1931 is almost free, 2027

Tonight is the last day of copyright for US works published in 1931. Their 95-year term ends on 31 December 2026, so on 1 January 2027 they enter the US public domain. That includes some of the most famous films ever made.

The headliners

  • Dracula (Tod Browning, with Bela Lugosi)
  • Frankenstein (James Whale, with Boris Karloff)
  • City Lights (Charlie Chaplin)
  • M (Fritz Lang)
  • The Public Enemy (with James Cagney)
  • The Maltese Falcon (the 1931 version), Monkey Business (the Marx Brothers), The Front Page, Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde and more

We track 33 films of this class in a free dataset: class-1931.json and class-1931.csv (CC0, no attribution needed).

What becomes free

The 1931 film itself: the images and the original soundtrack as released. From tomorrow anyone in the US can copy, share, screen, remix and sell those films without permission.

What does not become free

  • Trademarks. Universal still uses its monster characters as brands. A free film is not a free logo or product line. Mickey Mouse in 2024 showed how this works.
  • Later versions. Remakes, sequels, restorations with new work and colorized versions keep their own copyrights.
  • Modern recordings. A new score added to a copy you download is a separate, protected recording.
  • Other countries. This is US status only. Terms abroad are different.

The release-date trap

Not every "1931" film is on tomorrow's list. Little Caesar and Cimarron premiered in early 1931 but were registered for copyright in December 1930, so they already became free on 1 January 2026. The date on the copyright record is what counts, not the year on the poster.
