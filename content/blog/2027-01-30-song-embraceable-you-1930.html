George and Ira Gershwin wrote "Embraceable You" two years before anyone published it. Is the song public domain now? Yes. Here is what we found.

Is "Embraceable You" free to use?

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1930, so it became free on 1 January 2026 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record it or print the sheet music without permission.

Who wrote "Embraceable You"?

George Gershwin wrote the music and Ira Gershwin wrote the lyrics. Wikipedia states it was written in 1928 for an unproduced operetta, "East Is West," but was not published until 1930 by Harms, Inc., when it was used in the Broadway musical "Girl Crazy." Because the publication year, not the writing year, starts the US copyright clock, 1930 is the year that counts. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1930, that is 2026.

Can you use someone else's recording of "Embraceable You"?

A free song does not make every recording free. A sound recording has its own copyright, with its own clock. Billie Holiday's 1944 recording, inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2005, is a separate, still-protected work, even though the song itself is not.
