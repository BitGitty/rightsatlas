Is the 1916 Sherlock Holmes film public domain? Is the 1916 Sherlock Holmes film public domain? Yes. Essanay's seven-reel feature, the only filmed record of William Gillette's famous stage Holmes, lapsed decades ago, long before the print itself resurfaced in 2014.

Why is Sherlock Holmes public domain?

Sherlock Holmes (1916). Essanay Film Manufacturing Company released the film on May 15, 1916. Under the pre-1978 copyright regime, its initial 28-year term would have expired by 1944 at the latest, and Essanay had already dissolved by 1918, leaving no one to file a renewal. Independently, every US work published in 1930 or earlier is public domain by the bright-line term-expiry rule, which covers this 1916 release either way. See the Cornell Copyright Information Center guide to copyright term and the public domain.

Is the soundtrack of Sherlock Holmes public domain too?

  • Story: public domain. The film adapts Gillette's own stage play, first published in authorized text form in 1922, which itself drew on Arthur Conan Doyle stories published between 1887 and 1894. Every layer of source material predates 1930 and is public domain by term.
  • Score: partly protected. Essanay made no synchronized score for this 1916 silent film. But the San Francisco Silent Film Festival's 2015 theatrical restoration was shown with new, recorded accompaniment by the Donald Sosin Ensemble, which is separately copyrighted. Copies without that accompaniment are free of the complication.
  • Trademark: not verified. The Conan Doyle estate has a history of asserting rights over the Holmes character, including a 2014 copyright case it lost. Whether it or anyone else holds a live US trademark on "Sherlock Holmes" for entertainment goods has not been checked in USPTO records.
  • Restorations: partly protected. A faithful restoration of public-domain footage adds no new US copyright, so the San Francisco Silent Film Festival's restored images are as free as the original film. The Sosin Ensemble's recorded score attached to that restoration is a separate, protected post-1978 work.

Where does Sherlock Holmes come from?

According to Wikipedia, William Gillette stars as Holmes in this adaptation of his own 1899 stage play, battling Professor Moriarty over letters stolen from Alice Faulkner. Produced by Essanay in Chicago, the film was believed lost for nearly a century until a nitrate print turned up at the Cinémathèque Française in Paris in October 2014 and was restored for public viewing.
