Is Body and Soul public domain? Yes. Oscar Micheaux's 1925 drama, Paul Robeson's first film, is free to use in the US.

Why the film print is free

Micheaux published the film through his own Micheaux Film Corporation on November 9, 1925. Under US law, the maximum possible term for a 1925 work, 95 years from publication, expired on January 1, 2021. The AFI Catalog confirms the film is in the public domain, and no renewal could have changed that outcome. The film print is public domain.

The other layers

  • Score: partially protected. The 1925 print is silent and carries no score of its own. A 2016 restoration from Kino Lorber adds a newly composed score by DJ Spooky, which is a separate, fully protected work.
  • Story: public domain. Micheaux wrote it himself in 1925; even under a broader reading that it drew on Eugene O'Neill's 1921 play "The Emperor Jones," that source is also past its US term.
  • Trademark: not verified. We have not checked for any active marks tied to the Micheaux Film Corporation name.
  • Restorations: partially protected. Two restorations are in active distribution: the George Eastman Museum restoration on Criterion's 2007 Paul Robeson DVD collection, and the Kino Lorber restoration in its 2016 "Pioneers of African-American Cinema" set. A faithful restoration adds no new copyright by itself, but the Kino Lorber release is different, because its added DJ Spooky score is a new, separate work, and ripping either copy-protected disc carries its own legal risk under the DMCA.

A little history

Oscar Micheaux wrote, directed, produced and distributed the film himself. Paul Robeson, then 27, made his screen debut in a dual role, as an escaped convict posing as a preacher and as that preacher's honest twin brother. New York's censors rejected Micheaux's original nine-reel cut twice, and he had to shorten it to five reels before it could open; that longer version no longer survives, according to Wikipedia. What remains was added to the Library of Congress's National Film Registry in 2019.
