Most people know "Tiptoe Through the Tulips" from a ukulele and a falsetto, decades after the song itself was written. Here is what we found about its US copyright.

Is "Tiptoe Through the Tulips" free to use?

The song is public domain in the US. It was published in 1929, so it became free on 1 January 2025 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record a new version, or print the sheet music without asking permission.

Who wrote "Tiptoe Through the Tulips"?

Wikipedia states the song, with music by Joe Burke and lyrics by Al Dubin, was published in 1929 by M. Witmark & Sons, written for the film Gold Diggers of Broadway (1929). A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1929, that is 2025.

Can you use someone else's recording of "Tiptoe Through the Tulips"?

A free song does not free every recording of it. Each recording carries its own, separate copyright on its own clock. If you need the song for a video or a new release, perform or record it yourself.

Where do people know "Tiptoe Through the Tulips" from?

Tiny Tim sang it on Rowan & Martin's Laugh-In on 5 February 1968; his single, released that April, reached number 17 on the Billboard Hot 100 (source).
