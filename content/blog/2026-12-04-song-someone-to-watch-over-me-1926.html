Is "Someone to Watch Over Me" public domain? Yes. The George and Ira Gershwin standard has been free in the US since 1 January 2022 at the latest. Anyone can now perform it, record it, or print the sheet music without permission.

The evidence

The music is by George Gershwin and the lyrics are by Ira Gershwin. Wikipedia's infobox gives the publication date as 3 November 1926, by Harms, Inc., written for the musical "Oh, Kay!" which opened that same year. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1926, that is 2022, so the song has now been free for several years, and sheet music publishers, choirs and cover artists no longer need anyone's permission to use it.

Recordings are a different right

A free composition does not make a recording of it free. See our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights: a sound recording runs on its own clock, so any modern recording of this song stays protected even though the 1926 composition itself is not.

Where people know it from

The song was introduced in "Oh, Kay!" (1926), sung by Gertrude Lawrence while holding a rag doll in a sentimental solo scene (source).
