Is "When the Moon Comes over the Mountain" public domain? Yes, as of 1 January 2027. Kate Smith's theme song just finished its 95-year US copyright term, along with the rest of the class of 1931.

The evidence

The song was written by Harry Woods and Howard Johnson, and published on 28 February 1931 by Robbins Music Corp., according to Wikipedia. Its copyright was renewed in 1958, which confirms it stayed under active copyright for the decades in between, right up to its natural 95-year expiry. A song published in year Y is free in the US from 1 January of Y + 96 at the latest. For 1931, that is 2027, so the song is now free since 1 January 2027 at the latest, right alongside the rest of the US class of 1931.

Recordings are a different right

A free composition does not free any particular recording of it. See our explainer on why a song and a recording are different copyrights. Kate Smith's own performances of her theme song remain separate, protected sound recordings, each on its own clock, regardless of how the underlying 1931 song's own term ran out.

Where people know it from

The song became Kate Smith's signature theme (source), opening her radio broadcasts for years.
