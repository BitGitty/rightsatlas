About RightsAtlas

RightsAtlas tells you, for free, whether a classic film or song is public domain in the US: free for anyone to watch, share, copy or reuse. Every answer shows its sources, so you can check it yourself.

Why we built it

Many websites say "this film is public domain" without proof, and many of them are wrong. A film is also not one single thing: the pictures can be free while the music or the story is still owned by someone. So we check each part separately and show the evidence for each one.

What you can do here

  • Films: check if a classic film is free, and watch it free when it is.
  • Songs: check if a song is free. A recording of a song is a separate right, and we explain both.
  • Tools: a public domain calculator, a copyright record finder, a free movie picker, and what becomes free each year.
  • Blog: a short, sourced post every day.
  • Search: find any film or song we have checked (the 🔎 button at the top of every page).

How we check

We look up the year a work was published, the official US copyright records (for example the renewal records in the Catalog of Copyright Entries), and the law. Our site will not publish a "verified" answer without a source. Each page shows the date we last checked it. Read how we check, step by step.

Who makes it

RightsAtlas is made by Bit Git, a public domain researcher who also makes a classic-film video channel. We use AI tools to help gather research and run the site, and we say so openly. The answers come from the sources and rules shown on each page, not from an AI's opinion.

Important

This is research, not legal advice. It covers the United States only; other countries have different rules. Before you use a work commercially, check it yourself.

Found a mistake?

Please tell us, ideally with a source. We fix errors and list every fix on our corrections page. Journalists can ask us for our free Public Domain Day dataset.
